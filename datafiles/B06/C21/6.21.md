# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 6, CHAPTER 21

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 1</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ६.२१ एकविंशोऽध्यायः । राजपुत्र्याः एकावल्याः वर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच ।
> जातकर्मादिसंस्कारांश्चकार नृपतिस्तदा ।
> दिने दिने जगामाशु वृद्धिं बालः सुलालितः ॥ १ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! In the meanwhile the King Turvasu performed the Jātakarma (a religious ceremony performed at the birth of a child) and other ceremonies of the child. The boy was nurtured duly and began to grow older day by day.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> नृपः संसारजं प्राप्य सुखं पुत्रसभुद्भवम् ।
> ऋणत्रयविमोक्षं च मेने तेन महात्मना ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King began to enjoy his worldly life on getting this son and thought within himself that the boy had freed him from the three debts due to the Fathers, the Ṛṣis and the Devas.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 3</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> षष्ठेऽन्नप्राशनं तस्य कृत्वा मासि यथाविधि ।
> तृतीयेऽथ तथा वर्षे चूडाकरणमुत्तमम् ॥ ३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Next, in the sixth month, the King performed the Annaprāsana ceremony (putting the boiled rice in the mouth of the child) and in the third year performed regularly his Cūḍa Karaṇa (the ceremony of the first tonsure) ceremony.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> चकार ब्राह्मणान् द्रव्यैः सम्पूज्य विविधैर्धनैः ।
> गोभिश्च विविधैर्दानैर्याचकानितरानपि ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He distributed on those occasions various articles, wealth and cows to the Brāhmaṇas and other articles to various other mendicants and made them glad.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वर्षे चैकादशे तस्य मौञ्जीबन्धनकर्म वै ।
> कारयित्वा धनुर्वेदमध्यापयत पार्थिवः ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

In the eleventh year, he performed the boy’s holy thread (Upanayana) ceremony and tying the girdle made of a triple string of Muñja grass and put the boy to learn archery.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अधीतवेदं पुत्रं तं राजधर्मविशारदम् ।
> दृष्ट्वा तस्याभिषेकाय मतिं चक्रे जनाधिपः ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Next when the boy passed off proficiently in the study of the Vedas and in learning the kingly duties, the King desired to install him on the throne.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पुष्यार्कयोगसंयुक्ते दिवसे नृपसत्तमः ।
> कारयामास सम्भारानभिषेकार्थमादरात् ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King Turvasu then collected with great care all the necessary articles for installation in an auspicious day, the combination of Puṣyā asterism and Arka Yoga.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 8</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> द्विजानाहूय वेदज्ञान् सर्वशास्त्रविचक्षणान् ।
> अभिषेकं चकारासौ विधिवत् स्वात्मजस्य ह ॥ ८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He called then the Brāhmins, well versed in the Vedas and in the Śāstras, and became ready, in accordance with due rites, to perform the installation ceremony of the prince.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> जलमानीय तीर्थेभ्यः सागरेभ्यश्च पार्थिवः ।
> स्वयं चकार विथिवदभिषेकं शुभे दिने ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Waters were brought from various sacred places of pilgrimage and from the several oceans and on an auspicious day the King performed himself the installation of his son.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 10</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> धनं दत्त्वाथ विप्रेभ्यो राज्यं पुत्रे निवेश्य सः ।
> जगाम वनमेवाशु स्वर्गकामः स भूपतिः ॥ १० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When the ceremony was over the King gave away hoards of wealth to the Brāhmins and giving the charge of his kingdom to his son, he went to the forest with a desire to ascend to the Heavens.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एकवीरं नृपं कृत्वा सम्मान्य सचिवानथ ।
> भार्यया सह भूपालः प्रविवेश वनं वशी ॥ ११ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Thus placing Ekavīra on the throne, the King Turvasu shewed respects to his ministers, and, controlling his senses went to the forest accompanied by his wife.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 12</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मैनाकशिखरे राजा कृत्वा तार्तीयमाश्रमम् ।
> नित्यं पत्रफलाहारश्चिन्तयामास पार्वतीम् ॥ १२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On the top of the Maināka mountain he took up the vow of Vānaprastha and sustaining his life on leaves and fruits began to meditate Pārvatī.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 13-14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एवं स नृपतिः कृत्वा दिष्टान्ते सह भार्यया ।
> मृतोऽसौ वासवं लोकं गतः पुण्येन कर्मणा ॥ १३ ॥
> 
> इन्द्रलोकं पिता प्राप्त इति श्रुत्वाथ हैहयः ।
> चकार वेदनिर्दिष्टं कर्म चैवौर्ध्वदैहिकम् ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Thus when his Prārabdha Karma ended, he left his mortal coil with his wife and went by virtue of his good deeds to the Indraloka. Hearing that the King had ascended to Heavens, his son Ekavīra Haihaya performed his funeral ceremonies according to the rules laid down by the Vedas.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 15</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कृत्वोत्तराः क्रियाः सर्वाः पितुः पार्थिवनन्दनः ।
> राज्यं चकार मेधावी पित्रा दत्तं सुसम्मतम् ॥ १५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King’s son, the intelligent Haihaya, performed, one after another, all the ceremonies due and began to govern the kingdom which was free from enemies.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

The virtuous King Ekavīra remained duly obedient to his ministers after he got possession of his kingdom and began to enjoy all the best things.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 17</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एकस्मिन् दिवसे राजा मन्त्रिपुत्रैः समन्वितः ।
> जगाम जाह्नवीतीरे हयारूढः प्रतापवान् ॥ १७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The powerful King one day went on horseback to the banks of the Ganges with the minister’s son.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सम्पश्यन् पादपान् रम्यान् कोकिलालापसंयुतान् ।
> पुष्पितान् फलसंयुक्तान् षट्पदालिविराजितान् ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Roaming about, he found there the boughs of trees had assumed a very graceful appearance, with loads of fruits, echoed with the sweet voice of the cuckoos and with the humming of the bees.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मुनीनामाश्रमान्दिव्यान् वेदध्वनिनिनादितान् ।
> होमधूमावृताकाशान् मृगशावसमावृतान् ॥ १९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Close by were the hermitages of the Munis, where the bucks were skipping about and at other places the Vedas were being chanted. The smoke was seen rising from the altars, where oblations were being offered and appeared to form like a black canopy in the Heavens.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> केदाराञ्छालिसम्पक्वान् गोपिकाभिः सुरक्षितान् ।
> प्रफुल्लपङ्कजारामान्निकुञ्जांश्च मनोरमान् ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Full ripe grains were enhancing the beauty of the fields and the cowherdesses were merrily watching the fields. Places of recreations adorned with full blown lotuses and beautiful groves were attracting the attention of the visitors.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 21</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रेक्षमाणः प्रियालांस्तु चम्पकान् पनसद्रुमान् ।
> बकुलांस्तिलकान्नीपान्मन्दारांश्च प्रफुल्लितान् ॥ २१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The various trees, Piyāla, Champaka, Panasa, Bakula, Tilaka, Kadamba and Mandāra, and others were adorned with fruits, stealing away the minds of the people.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 22</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शालांस्तालांस्तमालांश्च जम्बूचूतकदम्बकान् ।
> स गच्छञ्जाह्नवीतोये प्रफुल्लं शतपत्रकम् ॥ २२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

At other places, other trees Sal, Tamāla, Jack, Mango, Kali Kadamba, etc., stood gracefully. Next when the King went to the Ganges water, he saw the gay beautiful full blown lotuses were spreading their fragrant scents all around.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 23-27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पङ्कजं चातिगन्धाढ्यमपश्यदवनीपतिः ।
> दक्षिणे जलजस्याथ पार्श्वे कमललोचनाम् ॥ २३ ॥
> 
> कनकाभां सुकेशीं च कम्बुग्रीवां कृशोदरीम् ।
> बिम्बोष्ठी सुन्दरीं किञ्चित्समुद्यत्सुपयोधराम् ॥ २४ ॥
> 
> सुनासां चारुसर्वाङ्गीमपश्यत् कन्यकां नृपः ।
> रुदतीं तां सखीं त्यक्त्वा विह्वलां दुःखपीडिताम् ॥ २५ ॥
> 
> साश्रुनेत्रां क्रन्दमानां विजने कुररीमिव ।
> संवीक्ष्य राजा पप्रच्छ कन्यकां शोककारणम् ॥ २६ ॥
> 
> सुनसे ब्रूहि कासि त्वं कस्य पुत्री शुभानने ।
> गन्धर्वी देवकन्याथ कथं रोदिषि सुन्दरि ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On the right side of these lotuses, he saw a lotus-eyed girl. She was shining like the gold, her beautiful hairs were long and curling; her throat was like a Kambu, belly thin, lips like the Bimba fruits, several other limbs well built and graceful, breasts risen a little, nose beautiful and all her body was exquisitely lovely; that lady just blooming into youth was suffering bereavements from her comrades and was very distressed and seemed bewildered. She was crying like an ewe in a dense lonely forest. Seeing her, the King asked her what was the reason of her sorrows? O Cuckoo-voiced One! You are as yet a girl; who has left you alone in this state?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 28-29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कथमेकाकिनी बाले त्यक्ता केन पिकस्वरे ।
> पतिस्ते क्व गतः कान्ते पिता वा ब्रूहि साम्प्रतम् ॥ २८ ॥
> 
> किं ते दुःखमरालभ्रु कथयाद्य ममान्तिके ।
> करोमि दुःखनाशं ते सर्वथैव कृशोदरि ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Sweet One! Tell me where is your husband now or where is your father? O One looking askance! What is your trouble; explain it to me. O thin-bellied One! I will, no doubt, remove all your sorrows and troubles.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 30-31</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न राज्ये मम तन्वङ्गि पीडां कोऽपि करोत्यलम् ।
> न भयं चौरजं कान्ते न राक्षसभयं तथा ॥ ३० ॥
> 
> मयि शासति भूपाले नोत्पाता दारुणा भुवि ।

>
**English Translation:**

O fair-limbed One! In my dominion nobody ever gives trouble to any other body. O lovely One! There is no fear in my kingdom from thieves or Rākṣasas; or any fear from any serious dangerous calamities on this earth, fear from lions, tigers or any other dangers while my sway is predominant.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 32</b></summary>
  <br>

>
**English Translation:**

O One of beautiful thighs! Why are you crying on this lonely bank of the Ganges? Tell me what is your pain?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 33</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हन्म्यहं दुःखमत्युग्रं प्राणिनां पृथिवीतले ।

>
**English Translation:**

O Pure One! I can remove the pains and miseries, even of a serious nature, of men, whether they come from the Deva or human sources; and this is my principal vow.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 34-35</b></summary>

**English Translation:**

O Large-eyed One! Speak what is your inmost desire; I will carry it out instantaneously. When the king thus spoke, that beautiful woman spoke in gentle words :-- O King! Hear the cause of my sorrows.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 36</b></summary>

O King! Why will the people cry, to no purpose, unless calamities come before them? O Mighty-armed One! I now tell you why I am weeping.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

O King! There was a very religious King named Rabhya in another province that is not yours. At first he had no issue. He had a very beautiful wife named Rukmarekhā.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 38</b></summary>
  <br>

She was clever, chaste and endowed with all auspicious qualities. But issueless as she was, she remained very sorry and, in a remorseful tone, she spoke to her husband Raibbya :-- O Lord! I am barren; I have no sons; I am therefore a very unhappy creature. My life is in vain; what use is there in my living?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 39-40</b></summary>
  <br>

**English Translation:**

When the queen thus spoke very distressedly, the king called the Brāhmaṇas, versed in the Vedas, and began to perform an excellent sacrificial ceremony, in due accordance with the Vedic rules. With a desire to get a son, he made many presents in profuse quantities.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 41</b></summary>
  <br>

>
**English Translation:**

When copious quantities of ghee were offered as oblations, there arose, from the fire, a girl beautiful in all respects and endowed with all auspicious signs.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 42-43</b></summary>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 44-45</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हुताशनात्समुद्भूता होत्रा सा स्वीकृता तदा ।
> होता प्रोवाच राजानं गृहीत्वा तां सुमध्यमाम् ॥ ४४ ॥
> 
> राजन् पुत्रीं गृहाणेमां सर्वलक्षणसंयुताम् ।
> एकावलीव सम्भूता हूयमानाद्धुताशनात् ॥ ४५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When the girl arose from the fire, the priest (Hotā) took that lean and thin lady of a nice waist by her arms and presented her to the King and said :-- O King! Accept this daughter, endowed with all auspicious signs.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 46-47</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> नाम्ना चैकावली लोके ख्याता पुत्री भविष्यति ।
> सुखितो भव भूपाल पुत्र्या पुत्रसमानया ॥ ४६ ॥
> 
> सन्तोषं कुरु राजेन्द्र दत्ता देवेन विष्णुना ।
> होतुर्वाक्यं नृपः श्रुत्वा दृष्ट्वा तां कन्यकां शुभाम् ॥ ४७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When Homa was being performed, the daughter came up like the garland Ekāvalī; therefore this girl became famous in this world by the name Ekāvalī. O Ruler of the earth! Take this girl, resembling a son and be happy. O King! Viṣṇu, the Deva of the Devas, has given you this Jewel, this daughter; so be contented.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 48-49</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> जग्राह परमप्रीतो होत्रा दत्तां सुसम्मताम् ।
> गृहीत्वा नृपतिस्तां तु ददौ पत्न्यै वराननाम् ॥ ४८ ॥
> 
> आभाष्य रुक्मरेखायै गृहाण सुभगे सुताम् ।

>
**English Translation:**

Hearing thus the words of the priest, the King saw this good-looking girl and with gladdened heart took the beautiful daughter from his hands. Thus with that lovely daughter he went to his wife Rukmarekhā and said :-- O Beautiful One! Take this daughter. The queen Rukmarekhā felt the pleasure of having a son when she got in her arms that lotus eyed beautiful daughter.

</details>

<details>

>
**English Translation:**

The King next performed the natal and other ceremonies of the daughter and did all other acts as if she had been a son to him duly in accordance with the rules.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

The King performed his own sacrificial ceremonies and gave away lots of Dakṣiṇās to the Brāhmins and dismissed them and became very glad.

</details>

On that very day the birthday festival was performed as on the occasion of the birth of a son. And that daughter grew older, very affectionate and dear to all.

</details>

<details>

>
**English Translation:**

O Lovely One! You are a king and intelligent too; I will describe to you all the details; Hear. I am the daughter of the minister to that King.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 55</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> यशोवती च मे नाम समानं वय आवयोः ।
> वयस्याहं कृता राज्ञा क्रीडनाय तया सह ॥ ५५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

My name is Yaśovatī. That daughter and I look alike and of the same age. Therefore the king has made me her comrade. I spend my time day and night always with her as her constant dear companion.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 56-57</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सदा सहचरी जाता प्रेमयुक्ता दिवानिशम् ।
> एकावली गन्धवन्ति यत्र पद्मानि पश्यति ॥ ५६ ॥
> 
> तत्र सा रमते बाला नान्यत्र सुखमाप्नुयात् ।
> सुदूरे जाह्नवीतीरे भवन्ति कमलान्यपि ॥ ५७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Ekāvalī likes very much to remain and sport wherever she finds sweet-scented lotuses; at other places she does not find happiness. At the distant banks of the Ganges many lotuses grow; therefore Ekāvalī goes there with great pleasure to that place with me and her other fellow mates.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 58-59</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> रममाणा तत्र याता मत्समेता सखीयुता ।
> मया निवेदितं राजन् पुत्री ते कमलाकरान् ॥ ५८ ॥

> प्रेक्षमाणातिदूरे सा प्रयाति निर्जने वने ।

>
**English Translation:**

One day I told the King that Ekāvalī used to go daily to a distant solitary forest to see the lotus-lake. Then the King addressed her not to go and he got a lake built within the compounds of his palace and planted many lotus seeds therein.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 21 Verse 60</b></summary>

Gradually the lotuses began to blossom and the bees came there to drink honey. Still she used to go outside in search of lotuses. Then the King sent armed guards to accompany her.

<details>

  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तदा राज्ञा रक्षपालाः प्रेरिताः शस्त्रपाणयः ।
> एवं रक्षायुता तन्वी मत्समेता सखीयुता ।

> इति श्रीमद्देवीभागवते महापुराणेऽष्टादशसाहस्र्यां संहितायां

**English Translation:**

Thus that thin-bodied daughter of the King used to go daily to the banks of the Ganges for play, guarded by armed soldiers, accompanied by myself and other companions. Again when the sporting was over, she used to return to the palace. Here ends the Twenty-First Chapter on the Sixth Book on the installation of Ekavīra and the birth of Ekāvalī in the Mahāpurāṇam Śrī Mad Devi Bhāgavatam by Maharṣi Veda Vyāsa.
