# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 3, CHAPTER 29

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 1-2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ३.२९ एकोनत्रिंशोऽध्यायः । लक्ष्मणकृतरामशोकसान्त्वनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच ।
> तदाकर्ण्य वचो दुष्टं जानकी भयविह्वला ।
> वेपमाना स्थिरं कृत्वा मनो वाचमुवाच ह ॥ १ ॥
> 
> पौलस्त्य किमसद्वाक्यं त्वमात्थ स्मरमोहितः ।
> नाहं वै स्वैरिणी किन्तु जनकस्य कुलोद्भवा ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- Hearing these vicious words, Jānakī became very much confounded with fear and began to tremble; somehow collecting herself she began to say :-- “O descendant of the family of Pulastya! Why are you, prompted by lust, uttering these sinful words? I am born of the family of Janaka; therefore I cannot act wantonly according to my own inclination.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 3</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गच्छ लङ्कां दशास्य त्वं राम त्वां वै हनिष्यति ।
> मत्कृते मरणं तत्र भविष्यति न संशयः ॥ ३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O ten faced one! Better you go to Laṅkā quickly; else Rāmcandra will take away your life; you will no doubt iñcur death for my sake.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 4-5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> इत्युक्त्वा पर्णशालायां गता सा वह्निसन्निधौ ।
> गच्छ गच्छेति वदती रावणं लोकरावणम् ॥ ४ ॥
> 
> सोऽथ कृत्वा निजं रूपं जगामोटजमन्तिकम् ।
> बलाज्जग्राह तां बालां रुदती भयविह्वलाम् ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Thus saying, Sītā Devī went towards the Sacred fire called Gārhapatya, placed in the house, with words “go away” “go away” in her mouth. He, whose wickedness has caused all the Lokas cry out “save” “save”, the same Rāvaṇa, of perverted intellect, then assumed his real form, went towards the hut and caught hold of Sītā Devī who was crying, bewildered with fear.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> रामरामेति क्रन्दन्ती लक्ष्मणेति मुहुर्मुहुः ।
> गृहीत्वा निर्गतः पापो रथमारोप्य सत्वरः ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Sītā cried “Rāma” “Rāma” “Lakṣmaṇa”, and the sinful Rāvaṇa caught hold of her and quickly mounting her on the chariot, fast got away.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 7-9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गच्छन्नरुणपुत्रेण मार्गे रुद्धो जटायुषा ।
> सङ्ग्रामोऽभून्महारौद्रस्तयोस्तत्र वनान्तरे ॥ ७ ॥
> 
> हत्वा तं तां गृहीत्वा च गतोऽसौ राक्षसाधिपः ।
> लङ्कायां क्रन्दती तात कुररीव दुरात्मनः ॥ ८ ॥
> 
> अशोकवनिकायां सा स्थापिता राक्षसीयुता ।
> स्ववृत्तान्नैव चलिता सामदानादिभिः किल ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On the way Jatāyu, the son of Aruṇa, met Rāvaṇa; and a terrible fight then ensued between the two, when the evil minded Rāvaṇa, the king of the Demons, killed Jatāyu. Rāvaṇa carried Sītā to Laṅkā. Then Sītā cried like a forlorn deer and Rāvaṇa kept her in the Aśoka forest (Jaffna), surrounded and guarded by the Rākṣasis. The king of Laṅkā tempted Sītā with comforting words, and the kingdoms, etc., but she never swerved from her own pure and stainless chastity.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 10-12</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> रामोऽपि तं मृगं हत्वा जगामादाय निर्वृतः ।
> आयान्तं लक्ष्मणं वीक्ष्य किं कृतं तेऽनुजासमम् ॥ १० ॥
> 
> एकाकिनीं प्रियां हित्वा किमर्थं त्वमिहागतः ।
> श्रुत्वा स्वनं तु पापस्य राघवस्त्वब्रवीदिदम् ॥ ११ ॥
> 
> सौमित्रिस्त्वब्रवीद्वाक्यं सीतावाग्बाणपीडितः ।
> प्रभोऽत्राहं समायातः कालयोगान्न संशयः ॥ १२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On the other side, Rāmacandra after killing the deer and taking it was coming back calmly, when he saw Lakṣmaṇa going to him and said “O Lakṣmaṇa! What a great blunder you have committed! Hearing the voice of that villain conjurer, how is it that you have left my dear Sītā alone and come here!” Lakṣmaṇa said :-- “O Lord! Being pierced sharply by Sītā Devī’s words (coming like sharpened arrows) and being driven away by her, under the guidance of the Inevitable Destiny as it were, I have come here; there is no doubt in this.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 13</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तदा तौ पर्णशालायां गत्वा वीक्ष्यातिदुःखितौ ।
> जानक्यन्वेषणे यत्नमुभौ कर्तुं समुद्यतौ ॥ १३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

They, then, both hurriedly went to their hut, made of leaves; and there not finding Sītā, they were very much afflicted with sorrows and went in quest of Jānakī.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मार्गमाणौ तु सम्प्राप्तौ यत्रासौ पतितः खगः ।
> जटायुः प्राणशेषस्तु पतितः पृथिवीतले ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa in their search for Sītā, came at last to the spot where Jatāyu, the king of birds, was lying on the surface of the earth, with his life ultimately on the point of parting away from his body.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 15-16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तेनोक्तं रावणेनाद्य हृताऽसौ जनकात्मजा ।
> मया निरुद्धः पापात्मा पातितोऽहं मृधे पुनः ॥ १५ ॥
> 
> इत्युक्त्वाऽसौ गतप्राणः संस्कृतो राघवेण वै ।
> कृत्वौर्घ्वदैहिकं रामलक्ष्मणौ निर्गतौ ततः ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Jatāyu said :-- Rāvaṇa, the king of Laṅkā, carried away today stealthily Sītā Devī; I resisted that villain who then fought with me on that account and threw me down on this spot by weapons. Thus saying, the king of birds died; whereupon Rāmcandra performed the burning of his dead body as well his funeral ceremonies. Then both of them went out of that place.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 17</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कबन्धं घातयित्वासौ शापाच्चामोचयत्प्रभुः ।
> वचनात्तस्य हरिणा सख्यं चक्रेऽथ राघवः ॥ १७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Then the Lord Rāmacandra killed Kabandha and freed him from his curse; and, through his advice, he made friendship with Sugrīva, the king of the monkeys, and was thus bound under a tie.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हत्वा च वालिनं वीरं किष्किन्धाराज्यमुत्तमम् ।
> सुग्रीवाय ददौ रामः कृतसख्याय कार्यतः ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Next Rāma killed the hero Balī as a duty and gave the excellent kingdom of Kiṣkindhyā to his new friend Sugrīva according to his promise.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तत्रैव वार्षिकान्मासांस्तस्थौ लक्ष्मणसंयुतः ।
> चिन्तयञ्जानकीं चित्ते दशाननहृतां प्रियाम् ॥ १९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Then, he began to ceaselessly think of the stealing away of Sītā by Rāvaṇa and passed away the four months of the rainy season there with his brother Lakṣmaṇa.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> लक्ष्मणं प्राह रामस्तु सीताविरहपीडितः ।
> सौमित्रे कैकयसुता जाता पूर्णमनोरथा ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Rāma, being very much shaken on account of the bereavement of Sītā, began to address Lakṣmaṇa thus :-- “O Saumitre! The desires of the daughter of the king of Kekaya are now fulfilled.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 21</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न प्राप्ता जानकी नूनं नाहं जीवामि तां विना ।
> नागमिष्याम्ययोध्यायामृते जनकनन्दिनीम् ॥ २१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Jānakī will no more be obtained; without Jānakī I will not go back to Ayodhyā; without Jānakī I won’t be able to live any longer.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 22</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गतं राज्यं वने वासो मृतस्तातो हृता प्रिया ।
> पीडयन्मां स दुष्टात्मा दैवोऽग्रे किं करिष्यति ॥ २२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Kingdom lost, dwelling in forests happened, father left his body, at last the dear wife is lost; the cruel hands of Destiny are tormenting me now thus; what more it will inflict, how can I say now?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> दुर्ज्ञेयं भवितव्यं हि प्राणिनां भरतानुज ।
> आवयोः का गतिस्तात भविष्यति सुदुःखदा ॥ २३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Brother Lakṣmaṇa! What is to happen is very hard to be known beforehand by men; I cannot say, what is written on my fate after this, painful or otherwise.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्राप्य जन्म मनोर्वंशे राजपुत्रावुभौ किल ।
> वनेऽतिदुःखभोक्तारौ जातौ पूर्वकृतेन च ॥ २४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

See! Both of us, the descendants of Manu, though born in a royal family, are exiled in forests due to our past deeds.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 25</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> त्यक्त्वा त्वमपि भोगांस्तु मया सह विनिर्गतः ।
> दैवयोगाच्च सौमित्रे भुङ्क्ष्व दुःखं दुरत्ययम् ॥ २५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Lakṣmaṇa! It is by Fate, too, that you, abandoning the pleasures of the royal surroundings, have come out with me; and you, too, are now suffering heaps of dire troubles with me.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न कोऽप्यस्मत्कुले पूर्वं मत्समो दुःखभाङ्नरः ।
> अकिञ्चनोऽक्षमः क्लिष्टो न भूतो न भविष्यति ॥ २६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

No one in our family suffered so much as we are suffering; why we talk of our family! No human being was ever born or will ever take his birth that suffered or will suffer like me so many troubles, will be like me incapacitated and a penniless pauper.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> किं करोम्यद्य सौमित्रे मग्नोऽस्मि दुःखसागरे ।
> न चास्ति तरणोपायो ह्यसहायस्य मे किल ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Saumitre! I am drowned in the ocean of pains and troubles; What am I to do now? I have no means to cross this ocean; I am quite helpless, no doubt.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 28</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न वित्तं न बलं वीर त्वमेकः सहचारकः ।
> कोपं कस्मिन्करोम्यद्य भोगेस्मिन्स्वकृतेऽनुज ॥ २८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

No money, nor armies, O hero! you are my one and only one companion; O brother! On whom shall I be angry when I am suffering on account of my own deeds?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गतं हस्तगतं राज्यं क्षणादिन्द्रासनोपमम् ।
> वने वासस्तु सम्प्राप्तः को वेद विधिनिर्मितम् ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Alas! The kingdom that could have been compared in prosperity to the Indra Sabha, was almost obtained by me when, in an instant, I lost it and am now in exile in forest. Lakṣmaṇ! Who can ascertain what is in the womb of Destiny?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 30</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> बालभावाच्च वैदेही चलिता चावयोः सह ।
> नीता दैवेन दुष्टेन श्यामा दुःखतरां दशाम् ॥ ३० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Oh! That soft bodied Sītā, with her child like nature came out with us in this forest; but the inexorable Fate has now drowned her, that perfectly beautiful woman, into an ocean of sorrows, difficult to be crossed?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 31</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> लङ्केशस्य गृहे श्यामा कथं दुःखं भविष्यति ।
> पतिव्रता सुशीला च मयि प्रीतियुता भृशम् ॥ ३१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

That fair daughter of Janaka is extremely devoted to me; she is pure and holy. How will she be able to suffer troubles in the house of the king of Laṅkā!

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 32</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न च लक्ष्मण वैदेही सा तस्य वशगा भवेत् ।
> स्वैरिणीव वरारोहा कथं स्याज्जनकात्मजा ॥ ३२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Lakṣmaṇa! Sītā Devī will never come under the control of Rāvaṇa; how can that excellent chaste woman act like an ordinary public woman?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 33</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> त्यजेत्प्राणान्नियन्तृत्वे मैथिली भरतानुज ।
> न रावणस्य वशगा भवेदिति सुनिश्चितम् ॥ ३३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Lakṣmaṇa! Rest assured that in case Rāvaṇa exercises, out of his lordly position, any violence on Sītā, she will rather put an end to her life than come under his control.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 34</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मृता चेज्जानकी वीर प्राणांस्त्यक्ष्याम्यसंशयम् ।
> मृता चेदसितापाङ्गीं किं मे देहेन लक्ष्मण ॥ ३४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Lakṣmaṇa! And when Jānakī sacrifices her life, I will assuredly do the same; for, of what use, then is this body to me when that fair Sītā has gone away with her life?”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 35</b></summary>
  <br>

> लक्ष्मणः प्राह धर्मात्मा सान्त्वयन्नृतया गिरा ॥ ३५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

While the lotus eyed Rāmcandra was thus weeping and expressing his regrets and sorrows, the religious Lakṣmaṇa consoled him with the following sweet, truthful, words :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 36</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

> आनयिष्यामि वैदेहीं हत्वा तं राक्षसाधमम् ॥ ३६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

“O Hero of the heroes! Kindly cast aside this weakness and have patience; I will soon kill that villain demon Rāvaṇa and get you back your Sītā Devī.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 37</b></summary>
  <br>

>
**English Translation:**

The wise steady persons remain on account of their fortitude, unshaken in their hearts whether in joy or in sorrow; whereas men, of little intellect, indulge in sorrows when they are happy.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 38</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

Coming in union and going out in disunion, both are under the hands of Destiny; What, then, there is the need for expressing sorrows for this body, which is not soul.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 39</b></summary>
  <br>

> तथा काले समीचीने संयोगोऽपि भविष्यति ॥ ३९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

As we have been banished from our kingdom into this forest, as there has happened this bereavement of Sītā, so, in proper time, we will again get back Sītā Devī.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 40</b></summary>
  <br>

>
**English Translation:**

O Darling of Jānakī! There must come a time when sorrows will be converted into happiness and vice versa; there will be nothing otherwise. So avoid this sorrow now and have firmness.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 29 Verse 41</b></summary>
  <br>

> शुद्धिं जनकनन्दिन्या आनयिष्यन्ति ते किल ॥ ४१ ॥
