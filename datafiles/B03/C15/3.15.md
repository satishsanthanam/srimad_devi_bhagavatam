# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 3, CHAPTER 15

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 1</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ३.१५ पञ्चदशोऽध्यायः । मनोरमया भारद्वाजाश्रमं प्रति गमनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच ।
> संयुगे च सति तत्र भूपयोराहवाय समुपात्तशस्त्रयोः ।
> क्रोधलोभवशयोः समं ततः सम्बभूव तुमुलस्तु विमर्दः ॥ १ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O king! When the war was declared, the two kings, excited by greed and anger, took up arms; and a dreadful encounter ensued.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> संस्थितः स समरे धृतचापः पार्थिवः पृथुलबाहुयुधाजित् ।
> संयुतः स्वबलवाहनादिकैराहवाय कृतनिश्चयो नृपः ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On one side the king Yudhājit of long arms, surrounded by his own army, with bows and arrows came ready to fight.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 3</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वीरसेन इह सैन्यसंयुतः क्षात्रधर्ममनुसृत्य सङ्गरे ।
> पुत्रिकात्मजहिताय पार्थिवः संस्थितः सुरपतेः समतेजाः ॥ ३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On the other hand, the fiery Vīrasena, the second God of the Devas appeared in the battle, following a true Kṣattriya custom, on behalf his daughter’s son.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> स बाणवृष्टिं विससर्ज पार्थिवो
> युधाजितं वीक्ष्य रणे स्थितञ्च ।
> गिरिं तडित्वानिव तोयवृष्टिभिः
> क्रोधान्वितः सत्यपराक्रमोऽसौ ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Then that truthful king Vīrasena, seeing Yudhājit in battle, became very angry and hurled arrows on him, as a cloud rains on the mountain tops.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तं वीरसेनो विशिखैः शिलाशितैः
> समावृणोदाशुगमैरजिह्मगैः ।
> चिच्छेद बाणैश्च शिलीमुखानसौ
> तेनैव मुक्तानतिवेगपातिनः ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On being covered, as it were, by the sharp and swift arrows, sharpened on a stone slab hurled at him by Vīrasena, Yudhājit, too, quickly, shot arrows at Vīrasena and cut off all his arrows.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गजरथतुरगाणां सम्बभूवातियुद्धं
> सुरनरमुनिसङ्घैर्वीक्षितञ्चातिघोरम् ।
> विततविहगवृन्दैरावृतं व्योम सद्यः
> पिशितमशितुकामैः कामगृध्रादिभिश्च ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O King! A dreadful fight then ensued between the cavalries, the warriors on the elephants; and the Devas, men, and Munis began to witness this terrible battle with wonder and astonishment. Birds, vultures and crows, desirous to eat the flesh of the dead soldiers, flew in the air.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तत्राद्भुता क्षतजसिन्धुरुवाह घोरा
> वृन्देभ्य एव गजवीरतुरङ्गमाणाम् ।
> त्रासावहा नयनमार्गगता नराणां
> पापात्मनां रविजमार्गभवेव कामम् ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Blood of elephants, horses and warriors, the bodies that lay dead, flowed in torrents awfully like rivers in that deadly battle ground. The torrent of blood excited fear amongst those who came to see it, as the river Vaitaraṇi on the way to hell (the Lord of Death) is very fearful to the sinners.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 8</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कीर्णानि भिन्नपुलिने नरमस्तकानि
> केशावृतानि च विभान्ति यथैव सिन्धौ ।
> तुम्बीफलानि विहितानि विहर्तुकामै-
> र्बालैर्यथा रविसुताप्रभवैश्च नूनम् ॥ ८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

human skulls were driven ashore by the current and they look like so many hollow shells of gourds scattered there for the play of the boys on the banks of the Jumnā.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वीरं मृतं भुवि गतं पतितं रथाद्वै
> गृध्रः पलार्थमुपरि भ्रमतीति मन्ये ।
> जीवोऽप्यसौ निजशरीरमवेक्ष्य कान्तं
> काङ्क्षत्यहोऽतिविवशोऽपि पुनः प्रवेष्टुम् ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When any warrior lay dead on the field, the vultures began to fly about in the air for devouring his flesh. It seemed then that the soul of the warrior beholding his beautiful body tried to reenter into his body, though he thought that it had become very inaccessible to him.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 10</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> आजौ हतोऽपि नृवरः सुविमानरूढः
> स्वाङ्के स्थितां सुरवधूं प्रवदत्यभीष्टम् ।
> पश्याधुना मम शरीरमिदं पृथिव्यां
> बाणाहतं निपतितं करभोरु कान्तम् ॥ १० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Some warriors on being slain in the battle instantly arose in a celestial car to the heavens and was seen addressing the celestial nymph, who came already within his embrace, thus “O one of beautiful thighs. Behold! how my beautiful body is lying on the earth below!”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एको हतस्तु रिपुणैव गतोऽन्तरिक्षं
> देवाङ्गनां समधिगम्य युतो विमाने ।
> तावत्प्रिया हुतवहे सुसमर्प्य देहं
> जग्राह कान्तमबला सबला स्वकीया ॥ ११ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Another warrrior thus slain got up in the heavens on a celestial car, came in possession of a celestial nymph and when he was sitting with her in the car, his former wife in the earth made herself a sati and burnt herself up in the funeral pyre, thus got a celestial body, came up to the heavens; and that chaste virtuous woman drew away perforce her own husband away from that celestial nymph.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 12</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> युद्धे मृतौ च सुभटौ दिवि सङ्गतौ ता-
> वन्योन्यशस्त्रनिहतौ सह सम्प्रयातौ ।
> तत्रैव जघ्नतुरलं परमाहितास्त्रा-
> वेकाप्सरोऽर्थविहतौ कलहाकुलौ च ॥ १२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Two warriors, went up, slew each other and lay down dead at the same time. They went up in the heavens at the same time and there began quarrel with each other and fight with their weapons for one and the same celestial nymph.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 13</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कश्चिद्युवा समधिगम्य सुराङ्गनां वै
> रूपाधिकां गुणवतीं किल भक्तियुक्तः ।
> स्वीयान् गुणान्प्रविततान्प्रवदंस्तदाऽसौ
> तां प्रेमदामनुचकार च योगयुक्तः ॥ १३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Some hero got in the heavens a nymph more lovely and beautiful than himself and he thus became very much attached and devoted her. He began to describe his own heroic qualities and also to copy dotedly the qualities of his lover so that she might remain faithfully attached to him.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> भौमं रजोऽतिविततं दिवि संस्थितञ्च
> रात्रिं चकार तरणिञ्च समावृणोद्यत् ।
> मग्नं तदेव रुधिराम्बुनिधावकस्मात्
> प्रादुर्बभूव रविरप्यतिकान्तियुक्तः ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The dust, arising from the dreadful encounter of the soldiers in battle field, rose up in the air and covered the sun. It appeared night. After a while that dust became absorbed in the blood below, and the sun appeared very red, reflected with the colour of the blood.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 15</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कश्चिद्गतस्तु गगनं किल देवकन्यां
> सम्प्राप्य चारुवदनां किल भक्तियुक्ताम् ।
> नाङ्गीचकार चतुरो व्रतनाशभीतो
> यास्यत्ययं मम वृथा ह्यनुकूलशब्दः ॥ १५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Some Brahmacārī fought in the battle and was slain. He went up to the heavens; instantly a lovely eyed Devakanyā, a celestial nymph desired to select him as bridegroom with great devotion. But that clever man did not accept the offer, thinking that his vow of Brahmacarya would be thus broken.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 16-17</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सङ्ग्रामे संवृते तत्र युधाजित्पृथिवीपतिः ।
> जघान वीरसेनं तं बाणैस्तीव्रैः सुदारुणैः ॥ १६ ॥
> 
> निहतः स पपातोर्व्यां छिन्नमूर्धा महीपतिः ।
> प्रभग्नं तद्बलं सर्वं निर्गतं च चतुर्दिशम् ॥ १७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O King! Thus when the battle was deadly, the king Yudhājit shot a sharp, dreadful arrow at Vīrasena and severed his head from body. Vīrasena lay dead on the battle field and his army was routed. The soldiers fled away from the battle.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 18-19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मनोरमा हतं श्रुत्वा पितरं रणमूर्धनि ।
> भयत्रस्ताथ सञ्जाता पितुर्वैरमनुस्मरन् ॥ १८ ॥
> 
> हनिष्यति युधाजिद्वै पुत्रं मम दुराशयः ।
> राज्यलोभेन पापात्मा सेति चिन्तापराभवत् ॥ १९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hearing that her father was slain in the battle, Manoramā became very terrified and anxious. She then began to think that the vicious wicked king Yudhājit will surely slay her son, for kingdom’s sake and to satisfy his enmity with her father.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> किं करोमि क्व गच्छामि पिता मे निहतो रणे ।
> भर्ता चापि मृतोऽद्यैव पुत्रोऽयं मम बालकः ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

What shall I do now? My father is slain in the battle. My husband is no more. My child is a minor to-day. Where shall I go?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 21</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> लोभोऽतीव च पापिष्ठस्तेन को न वशीकृतः ।
> किं न कुर्यात्तदाविष्टः पापं पार्थिवसत्तमः ॥ २१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Greed is very sinful; who is there that cannot be bought over by love of gold? and what vicious act can there be, that cannot be done when actuated by covetousness?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 22</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पितरं मातरं भ्रातॄन्गुरून्स्वजनबान्धवान् ।
> हन्ति लोभसमाविष्टो जनो नात्र विचारणा ॥ २२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

A greedy man does not hesitate to kill his father, mother, spiritual guide, friends and others. There is no doubt in this.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अभक्ष्यभक्षणं लोभादगम्यागमनं तथा ।
> करोति किल तृष्णार्तो धर्मत्यागं तथा पुनः ॥ २३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

It is the inordinate love of worldly things that makes a man eat what is held unclean in society, that makes a man approach a woman who is unapproachable, and it is greed that makes a man discard his own religion and become an apostate.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न सहायोऽस्ति मे कश्चिन्नगरेऽत्र महाबलः ।
> यदाधारे स्थिता चाहं पालयामि सुतं शुभम् ॥ २४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

In this city I find none so powerful as I can remain there under his shelter and be able to rear up my child.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 25</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हते पुत्रे नृपेणाद्य किं करिष्याम्यहं पुनः ।
> न मे त्राताऽस्ति भुवने येन वै सुस्थिता ह्यहम् ॥ २५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

What can I do if the king Yudhājit slay my son? There is none in this world who can save me, and, counting on whose shelter, I can stay here without any anxiety.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सापि वैरयुता कामं सपत्नी सर्वदा भवेत् ।
> लीलावती न मे पुत्रे भविष्यति दयावती ॥ २६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

And this my co-rival wife Līlāvatī will always practise enmity with me. She will never shew mercy on my son.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> युधाजिति समायाते न मे निःसरणं भवेत् ।
> ज्ञात्वा बालं सुतं सोऽद्य कारागारं नयिष्यति ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When Yudhājit will arrive in this city, I will never be able to go out of it and he will to-day put my son in the prison on the pretext that he is a minor.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 28-29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> श्रूयते हि पुरेन्द्रेण मातुर्गर्भगतः शिशुः ।
> कृन्तितः सप्तधा पश्चात्कृतास्ते सप्त सप्तधा ॥ २८ ॥
> 
> प्रविश्य चोदरं मातुः करे कृत्वाल्पकं पविम् ।
> एकोनपञ्चाशदपि तेऽभवन्मरुतो दिवि ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

I heard that, in days of yore, Indra entered into the womb of his pregnant step mother with a small thunderbolt in his hand and divided the foetus into seven parts with that weapon, again each of these seven into seven parts again, thus the forty nine Maruts were born in the Heavens.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 30-31</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सपत्न्यै गरलं दत्तं सपत्न्या नृपभार्यया ।
> गर्भनाशार्थमुद्दिश्य पुरैतद्वै मया श्रुतम् ॥ ३० ॥
> 
> जातस्तु बालकः पश्चाद्देहे विषयुतः किल ।
> तेनासौ सगरो नाम विख्यातो भुवि मण्डले ॥ ३१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

I heard also that in ancient times one queen gave poison to destroy the foetus in the womb of her rival wife. When the child came out of the womb, he was celebrated by the name of Sagara (with poison) in this earth.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 32</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> जीवमानोऽथ भर्ता वै कैकेय्या नृपभार्यया ।
> रामः प्रव्राजितो ज्येष्ठो मृतो दशरथो नृपः ॥ ३२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The husband was alive, and still his queen Kaikeyi banished the eldest son of his king, Śrī Rāmcandra to the forest; and the king Daśaratha sacrificed his life for that very reason.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 33</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मन्त्रिणस्त्ववशाः कामं ये मे पुत्रं सुदर्शनम् ।
> राजानं कर्तुकामा वै युधाजिद्वशगाश्च ते ॥ ३३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The ministers no doubt wanted before to install my son as the king; but now they are not independent; they have now yielded themselves to the king Yudhājit.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 34</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न मे भ्राता तथा शूरो यो मां बन्धात्प्रमोचयेत् ।
> महत्कष्टञ्च सम्प्राप्तं मया वै दैवयोगतः ॥ ३४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

There is no brother of mine powerful enough to release me from my bondage; I see I have fallen into a great difficulty by the combination of unforeseen circumstances.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 35</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> उद्यमः सर्वथा कार्यः सिद्धिर्दैवाद्धि जायते ।
> उपायं पुत्ररक्षार्थं करोम्यद्य त्वरान्विता ॥ ३५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Though the success depends on Fate, still one should make an earnest effort. If one does not make any effort, fate also remains asleep. I will therefore soon make out a plan to save my son.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 15 Verse 36-38</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> इति सञ्चिन्त्य सा बाला विदल्लं चातिमानिनम् ।
> निपुणं सर्वकार्येषु चिन्त्यं मन्त्रिवरोत्तमम् ॥ ३६ ॥
> 
> समाहूय तमेकान्ते प्रोवाच बहुदुःखिता ।
> गृहीत्वा बालकं हस्ते रुदती दीनमानसा ॥ ३७ ॥
> 
> पिता मे निहतः सङ्ख्ये पुत्रोऽयं बालकस्तथा ।
> युधाजिद्बलवान् राजा किं विधेयं वदस्व मे ॥ ३८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O King! Thinking thus, that woman Manoramā called in private the best and very respectable minister Vidalla, who was intelligent and expert in everything, and holding the hands of her son and weeping, said humbly in a depressed spirit “O Minister! My father is slain in the battle field, this my son is a minor, and Yudhājit is a powerful king; consider all these and tell me what I should do now?”

</details>
