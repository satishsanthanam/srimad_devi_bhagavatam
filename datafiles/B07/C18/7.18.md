# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 7, CHAPTER 18

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 1-2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ७.१८ अष्टादशोऽध्यायः । हरिश्चन्द्रद्वारा वृद्धब्राह्मणाय धनदानप्रतिज्ञावर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच -
> कदाचित्तु हरिश्चन्द्रो मृगयार्थं वनं ययौ ।
> अपश्यद्रुदतीं बालां सुन्दरीं चारुलोचनाम् ॥ १ ॥
> 
> तामपृच्छन्महाराजः कामिनीं करुणापरः ।
> पद्मपत्रविशालाक्षि किं रोदिषि वरानने ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! Once on a time Hariścandra went out to the forest on an hunting excursion; and, while roaming to and fro, he saw that a very beautiful lady was crying. The King, seeing this, took pity on her and asked :-- “O Fair One! Why are you in this forest crying alone? O Large eyed One! Has someone pained you?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 3-4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> केनासि पीडितात्यर्थं किं ते दुःखं वदाशु मे ।
> का च त्वं विजने घोरे कस्ते भर्ता पिताथवा ॥ ३ ॥
> 
> न बाधते च राज्ये मे राक्षसोऽपि पराङ्गनाम् ।
> तं हन्मि तरसा कान्ते यस्त्वां सुन्दरि बाधते ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

What is the cause of your sorrow? Express this quickly before me. Why have you come here in this dreadful lonely forest? What are the names of your husband and your father? O Beautiful One! In My kingdom, no demon can give any trouble to another’s lady; I will immediately kill him who has given you this trouble.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 5-6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ब्रूहि दुःखं वरारोहे स्वस्था भव कृशोदरि ।
> विषये मम पापात्मा न तिष्ठति सुमध्यमे ॥ ५ ॥
> 
> इति तस्य वचः श्रुत्वा नारी तमब्रवीन्नृपम् ।
> प्रमृज्याश्रूणि वदनाद्धरिश्चन्द्रं नृपोत्तमम् ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O thin-bellied One! Be comfortable; do not weep; tell me why you are in this sorrowful state; know that no sinner can remain within my territory.” Hearing the words of the King, the lady wiped out her tears by her hand and began to say :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 7-8</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> नार्युवाच -
> राजन् मां बाधतेऽत्यर्थं विश्वामित्रो महामुनिः ।
> तपः करोति यद्घोरं मदर्थं कौशिको वने ॥ ७ ॥
> 
> तेनाहं दुःखिता राजन् विषये तव सुव्रत ।
> विद्धि मां कमनां कान्तां पीडितां मुनिना भृशम् ॥ ८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O King! I am Siddharupiṇī, of the nature of success; to get me, Viśvāmitra is practising terrible austerities. So these troubles have arisen from him, the son of Kauśika. O King! For this reason I am sorry in Your kingdom. O One of good vows! I am a gentle lovely Lady; still that Muni is giving me so much trouble.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 9-10</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> राजोवाच -
> स्वस्था भव विशालाक्षि न ते दुःखं भविष्यति ।
> तमहं वारयिष्यामि मुनिं तापपरायणम् ॥ ९ ॥
> 
> इत्याश्वास्य स्त्रियं राजा तरसा मुनिसन्निधौ ।
> नत्वा प्रणम्य शिरसा तमुवाच महीपतिः ॥ १० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King said :-- “O Large-eyed One! No longer you will have to suffer any more pains. Be patient. I will go and make the Muni desist from his tapasyā.” Thus comforting the lady, the King went hurriedly to the Muni Viśvāmitra and, bowing down to him said with clasped palms :-- O Maharṣi! Why are you ailing your body by this terrible severe austerity!

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 11-12</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> स्वामिन्किं क्रियतेऽत्यर्थं तपसा देहपीडनम् ।
> किमर्थं ते समारम्भो ब्रूहि सत्यं महामते ॥ ११ ॥
> 
> वाञ्छितं तव गाधेय करोमि सफलं किल ।
> उत्तिष्ठोत्तिष्ठ तरसा तपसालमतः परम् ॥ १२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Highly intelligent One! For what great noble cause are you practising this hard tapasyā; speak truly to me. O Son of Gādhi! I will fulfil your desires; there is no need of your practising this severe penance; please get out of it immediately.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 13-14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विषये मम सर्वज्ञ न कर्तव्यं सुदारुणम् ।
> लोकपीडाकरं घोरं तपः केनापि कर्हिचित् ॥ १३ ॥
> 
> इत्थं निषिध्य तं राजा विश्वामित्रं गृहं ययौ ।
> मनसा क्रोधमाधाय गतोऽसौ कौशिको मुनिः ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Maharṣi! You know everything; so what shall I say anything further? See! It ought not anyone to practise this extremely dreadful tapasyā, causing troubles to the people within my territory. Thus prohibited by the King Hariścandra, the Muni became very angry at his heart and went towards his own hermitage.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 15</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> स गत्वा चिन्तयामास नृपकृत्यमसाम्प्रतम् ।
> वसिष्ठस्य च संवादं तपसः प्रतिषेधनम् ॥ १५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King, too, went back to his palace. The Muni on his arrival at his hermitage, began to cogitate in his mind, “Why has the King unjustly desisted me from my tapasyā” and also the discussions that took place between him and Vaśiṣṭha.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कोपाविष्टेन मनसा प्रतीकारमथाकरोत् ।
> विचिन्त्य बहुधा चित्ते दानवं घोरविग्रहम् ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Viśvāmitra became very angry at his heart and ready to take the vengeance of this. He thought over on many points and created a terrible demon of a dreadful appearance in the form of a boar and sent it to the territory of the King Hariścandra.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 17</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रेषयामास तद्देशं विधाय सूकराकृतिम् ।
> सोऽतिकायो महाकालः कुर्वन्नादं सुदारुणम् ॥ १७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

That terrible boar, of huge body, entered into the kingdom, raising a dreadful sound. The guards became afraid at his terrible noise.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 18-19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> राज्ञश्चोपवने प्राप्तस्त्रासयन् रक्षकांस्तदा ।
> मालतीनां च खण्डानि कदम्बानां तथैव च ॥ १८ ॥
> 
> यूथिकानां च वृन्दानि कम्पयंश्च मुहुर्मुहुः ।

>
**English Translation:**

Entering into the forest, that boar began to whirl round and round and destroy the Mālati forest, at another place the Kadamba forest, and at others the Yūthikā forest.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 20-21</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> चम्पकान्केतकीखण्डान्मल्लिकानां च पादपान् ।
> करवीरानुशीरांश्च निचखान शुभान्मृदून् ॥ २० ॥
> 
> मुचुकुन्दानशोकांश्च बकुलांस्तिलकांस्तथा ।
> उन्मूल्य कदनं तत्र चकार सूकरो वने ॥ २१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

At other places he began to dig up the earth by his tusks and root out the Champaka, Ketakī, Mallikā and various other trees. At other places again, he rooted out nice gentle Uśīra, Karavīra, Muchukunda, Aśoka, Vakula, Tilaka and other trees and so massacred the nice gardens and forests. The forest guards, then, taking their weapons, rushed forward on that boar.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 22</b></summary>

>
**English Translation:**

Those that were making garlands and the florists became very distressed and uttered uproars of consternation.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 23-24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> बाणैः सन्ताड्यमानोऽपि यदा त्रस्तो न वै मृगः ।
> रक्षकान्पीडयामास कोलः कालसमद्युतिः ॥ २३ ॥

**English Translation:**

That boar, as if an incarnate of Death, though routed out with flights of arrows, could not be terrified; rather when he began to harass the guards very much, they became very much afraid and being very distressed took the refuge of the King and, trembling, said :-- “O King! Protect us. Protect us.” And they cried piteously.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 25-26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तानागतान्समालोक्य भयार्तान्भूपतिस्तदा ।

**English Translation:**

Seeing the guards terrified and distressed, the King asked them :-- Whom do you fear so much and why you are so distressed? Speak truly before me. O Guards! I do not fear the Deva nor the Demons; so tell me who has created this panic amongst you.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

I, no doubt, will send that vicious cheat unto the door of Death by this arrow, who has come against me in this world. What sort of enemy is that? What is his form? What is his power and where is he residing now; speak this quickly to me. Be that enemy a Deva or a Dānava, I will slay him immediately by the multitude of arrows.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 29-31</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मालाकारा ऊचुः -
> न देवो न च दैत्योऽस्ति न यक्षो न च किन्नरः ।
> कश्चित्कोलो महाकायो राजंस्तिष्ठति कानने ॥ २९ ॥
> 
> पुष्पवृक्षानतिमृदून्दन्तेनोन्मूलयत्यसौ ।
> विदीर्णं तद्वनं सर्वं सूकरेणातिरंहसा ॥ ३० ॥
> 
> विशिखैस्ताडितोऽस्माभिर्दृषद्भिर्लकुटैस्तथा ।
> न बिभेति महाराज हन्तुमस्मानुपाद्रवत् ॥ ३१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The Mālākāras said :-- The enemy is not a Deva, nor a Dānava, Yakṣa nor a Kinnara; it is a boar of a huge body that has entered into the forest. Very powerful, he is uprooting by his teeth all the beautiful flower trees; in fact, he is ruining all the gardens and forests. O King! We shot arrows on him, struck him with cudgels and hurled stones at him so much; yet he did not get a bit afraid; rather he turned back to kill us.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 32</b></summary>

**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! Hearing these words, the King’s fury knew no bounds and, immediately getting on horseback, he went towards the garden and forest.

</details>

<details>

Then the horsemen, elephant drivers, charioteers and infantry, all followed him. When the King went there, he saw the terrible boar, of a huge body, whirling round and round and making the peculiar sound in the forest; and he witnessed also the destroyed condition of the forest and became very angry.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 35-36</b></summary>

**English Translation:**

He then drew his bow and arrows and fell down on him to take away his life. Seeing the King coming angrily towards him with bow and arrows in his hands, the boar began to sound more terribly and ran forward before him. The King saw the boar coming towards him with his mouth wide opened and began to shower arrows upon him to kill him.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 37-38</b></summary>
  <br>

**English Translation:**

The boar immediately made those arrows useless, and very violently and quickly jumped and passed away, over the King. When the boar passed away, the King angrily drew his bow with great care and shot sharpened arrows at him.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 39-40</b></summary>

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 41-42</b></summary>

The soldiers then entered the forest and scattered hither and thither; the King alone pursued the boar. The sun entered unto the meridian; and the King came to be alone in a lonely forest.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

His horse was fatigued, and he, too, was tired of hunger and thirst. The boar went away out of sight. The King also missed his way in that dense jungle and became greatly absorbed with intense cares and anxieties.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 45-46</b></summary>

He then began to think, “Where shall I now go? There is none to help me in this dense jungle. Especially I don’t know the right path.” While he was thus thinking, he saw, all on a sudden, a river with clear water in that lonely forest.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 47-48</b></summary>

> 
> जलं पीत्वा नृपस्तत्र सुखमाप महीपतिः ।
> इत्येष नगरं गन्तुं दिग्भ्रमेणातिमोहितः ॥ ४८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He became much delighted to see the flowing river and, alighting from horseback, he drank that water and made the horse also drink it.

</details>

He became much relieved by drinking; and though he was much bewildered not to find the right track, he wanted now to go to his own city.

At this moment Viśvāmitra came up there in an old Brāhmin form; the King also looking at him bowed down to the Brāhmin garbed Viśvāmitra, who then spoke to the King :--

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 51</b></summary>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एकाकी विजने राजन् किं चिकीर्षितमत्र ते ।

**English Translation:**

“O King! Welfare be unto you! What for have you come here? O King! What object have you got in view in this lonely forest? Be calm and quiet and speak everything before me.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 52-53</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> राजोवाच -
> सूकरोऽतिमहाकायो बलवान्पुष्पकाननम् ।
> समुपेत्य ममर्दाशु कोमलान्पुष्पपादपान् ॥ ५२ ॥

> तं निवारयितुं दुष्टं करे कृत्वा च कार्मुकम् ।

**English Translation:**

The King said :-- “O Brāhmin! One powerful boar of a huge body entered into my garden and spoilt altogether all the gentle flower trees there. To desist that boar, I pursued him with bow in hand and went out of the city.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 54-55</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गतोऽसौ दृक्पथात्पापो मायावी क्वापि वेगवान् ।
> पृष्ठतोऽहमपि प्राप्तः सैन्यं क्वापि गतं मम ॥ ५४ ॥
> 
> क्षुधितस्तृषितश्चाहं सैन्यभ्रष्टस्त्विहागतः ।
> न जाने पुरमार्गं च तथा सैन्यगतिं मुने ॥ ५५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

That powerful boar, very swift and, as it were, a magician, has escaped my sight and gone away where I do not know. I pursued him and have come now to this place and I do not know where my soldiers have gone. O Muni! Now I am deprived of my men, I am hungry and thirsty.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 56-57</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पन्थानं दर्शय विभो व्रजामि नगरं प्रति ।
> ममात्र भाग्ययोगेन प्राप्तस्त्वं विजने वने ॥ ५६ ॥
> 
> अयोध्यादिपतिश्चाहं हरिश्चन्द्रोऽतिविश्रुतः ।
> राजसूयस्य कर्ता च वाञ्छितार्थप्रदः सदा ॥ ५७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

I do not know which is the road to my city; nor do I know where my soldiers have gone. O Dear Lord! It is to my great fortune that you have come in this lonely forest. Now I want to return to my home; kindly shew me the way. I have completed my Rājasūya sacrifice. I always give everyone whatever he wants. This is known to everybody.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 18 Verse 58</b></summary>

> **Sanskrit:**

> आगन्तव्यमयोध्यायां दास्यामि विपुलं धनम् ॥ ५८ ॥
>
> *Colophon:*
> इति श्रीमद्देवीभागवते महापुराणेऽष्टादशसाहस्र्यां संहितायां सप्तमस्कन्धे
> हरिश्चन्द्रद्वारा वृद्धब्राह्मणाय धनदानप्रतिज्ञावर्णनं नामाष्टादशोऽध्यायः ॥ ७.१८॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Dvīja! If you want money for your sacrifice, then come with me to Ayodhyā and I will give you abundance of wealth. I am Hariścandra, the famous King of Ayodhyā.” Here ends the Eighteenth Chapter of the Seventh Book on the origin of the quarrel between Hariścandra and Viśvāmitra in the Mahāpurāṇam Śrī Mad Devī Bhāgavatam, of 18,000 verses, by Maharṣi Veda Vyāsa.

</details>
