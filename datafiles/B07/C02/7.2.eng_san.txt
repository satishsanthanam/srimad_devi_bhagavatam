CHAPTER II

On the Piercing of the Eyes of Cyavana Muni

जनमेव जय चाच

मामाख्याहि महाभाग राजां वंर्णे सवित्तग्न ।

सूवी-नवयमुत्तानां धर्मज्ञानां विशेषतः ॥ १ ॥

Janamejaya said: O Highly Fortunate One! Kindly narrate in detail the spread of the families of those kings in the Solar line who were born and who were especially endowed with the knowledge of Dharma.

व्यास उचाच

शृण भारत वक्ष्यापि रविवंशस्य विस्तरम् ।

यथा श्रुतं मया पूर्वे नारदाप्रविस्तमान् ॥ 2 ॥

Vyāsa said: O Bhārata! I now speak to you exactly what I heard of yore, from Nārada, the best of the Rṣi, how the Solar race spread.

एकादा नारदः श्रीमान्सरत्याल्पतेरे रुषे ।

आजागामाओं पुण्ये विचार-संदेषच्छया मुनिनः ॥ 3 ॥

प्रणाथ्य शिरसा यादी तस्योः संशित्यतस्ता ।

तततस्वासनं दत्ता कृत्वाहर्णणमधारतृ ॥ 4 ॥

Once, on an occasion, the Muni Śrimān Nārada, on his tour, came at his will to my holy hermitage on the beautiful banks of the Sarasvati river. On seeing him I bowed down at his feet and then remained standing before him.

विविधत्पूर्ववित्या ततुष्कावान्चनं तिवद् ।

पातितोई भूमिश्रेष्ठ पूज्यस्यामानेने चे ॥ ५ ॥

I then gave him a seat and worshipped him with great esteem. I then said to him: "O Best of Munis! You are worshipped by the whole universe; my retreat is sanctified by your coming.

कथां कथय सर्वत्र राज्ञां चरितसंयुतान् ।

राजानो ये समाख्याताः सपतेप्रतिम्मानोः कुले ॥ 6 ॥

तेषामुवर्तितुला चरितं परमाद्धुतम् ।

श्रोत्कामोउम्यहं ब्रह्म-सुर्ववैशस्य विशतरम् ॥ 7 ॥

O All-knowing One! Kindly narrate the histories of the Kings that were famous in the family of the seventh Manu; they were unequalled as far as their

origin was concerned and their characters as well wonderful. Therefore I am very eager to know, in detail, the history of the Solar race.

समाजालाई मुनिश्रेष्ठ समास्यामसुर्वकम् ।

इति पृष्टो मया राजत्रादः परमार्थवित् ॥ 8 ॥

उवाच प्रहसन्धितः समाभाष्य मुद्राउ-वयम् ।

O Muni! Describe shortly or in detail as the circumstances may require." O King! When I made this question, Nārada, the knower of the Highest Reality, gladly smiled, and, addressing me, began to describe the history of the Solar race.

नारद उवाच

श्रणु सत्यवतीसूने राजां वेगमन्तुनम् ॥ 9 ॥

पावनं कर्णिकुखदं धर्मानाविद्धियतूनम् ।

बहाा पूर्वे जगत्कर्ता नाधियंकजसभ्यः ॥ १ ० ॥

विष्णोरिति पुराणेषु प्रसिद्धः परिकीर्तितः ।

सर्वतः सर्वकर्तासी स्वधभूः सर्वशिक्षणम् ॥ १ १ ॥

तपस्तवा स विश्वाता वर्णिणाम्बुतं पुरा ।

सृष्टिकामः जिलो ध्याना प्राप्य सक्रिनमुतनाम् ॥ १२ ॥

युज्ञतुर्पाद्यायास माननाशुभतक्षणान् ।

Nārada said: O son of Satyavati! The history of the royal families is very holy and pleasant to hear; the more so when they are heard, one acquires Dharma and wisdom; therefore do you hear. In ancient times, Brahmā sprang from the navel-lotus of Viṣṇu and created the world. This is well-known in every Purāṇa. That self born, all-powerful, all-knowing, the Doer of all, the Universal Soul practised Tapas in ancient times for Ayuta (ten thousand) years. By virtue of that Tapas, He got special powers to create the world. He meditated on the Auspicious Mother, and getting from Her the excellent powers, He created first the mind-born sons, all endowed with auspicious signs.

मतीश्व: प्रयिततक्षेपाभवल्छिरूकर्गी ॥ 13 ॥

तस्य पुत्रोत्त्रिविखाताः: कर्णय: सर्वेस्मात: ।

Of them, Marici became well known in this act of creation. His son Kaśyapa was respected by all and he was of great celebrity.

यदोदरील तत्यासन्यार्यां द्वसुस्ता: किल ॥ 14 ॥

देवा: सर्वे समुदाया दैत्य द्वक्षा प्रवाना: ।

पशव: पक्षिणश्व तस्मात्यस्थितु काययपी ॥ 15 ॥

देवानां प्रधितः सूर्यां विश्वस्वाजाम तस्य तु ।

He had thirteen views, all daughters of Dakṣa Prajāpati. The Devas, Daityas, Yakṣas Pannagas, beasts and birds all sprang from him. Therefore this creation is called the Kāśyapī creation. Amongst the Devas, the Sun is specially famous; his other name is Vivasvān.

तस्य पुत्र: स विख्यातो वैवस्यतमनुर्पे: ॥ 16 ॥

तस्य पुत्रतदीक्ष्वाकु: सूर्यवेगिविदयर्न: ।

नवाभवनुस्तासस्य मनोरिद्वाकुपूर्वजाः ॥ 17 ॥

तेषां नामानि राजेन्द्र शृष्णुधैकमन्ता: पुनः ।

इक्ष्वाकुर्व ताभाग्यो धूर: शयातिरेव च ॥ 18 ॥

नरिथन्तस्था प्रश्रुर्जगी विद्धु सपना: ।

कलष्ठ पृषध्छ नवैते मानवा: स्मृता: ॥ 19 ॥

इक्ष्वाकुस्तु मनो: पुत्र: प्रयम: संमाजवात ।

त्यय पूजराते चारीज्ञेछो विकूक्षितार्यवान् ॥ 20 ॥

नवानां वंशवित्तरां संक्षेपण निशापाय ।

ग्रूरणां मनुपुवाणां मनोरंतरजन्मान् ॥ 21 ॥

नाभागस्य तु पुत्रोभूद्वंशीयः प्रतपावान् ।

His son was named Vaivasvat Manu; he was a famous king. Besides, Manu had nine more sons. Iksvāku was the eldest. Their names are :(1) Nābhāga, (2) Dhṛṣṭa, (3) Śaryāti, (4) Narisyanta, (5) Prāṃśu, (6) Nrga, (7) Diṣṭa, (8) Karūsa, and (9) Rṣadhara. Iksyāku, the son of Manu was born first. He had one hundred sons, and Vikukṣi was the wise and the eldest of these. I am now narrating how the nine sons, born afterwards of Manu, spread their families. Ambarīṣa was the son of Nābhaṇa; he was very truthful, powerful, and religious.

धर्मः स्वसंधः प्रजापानन्तनतः ॥ 22 ॥

धृत्यु धर्धकं क्षत्र बह्यभूतमजायत ।

स्रैमागकातं स्मयबह्याकर्मेत तथा ॥ 23 ॥

He always governed his subject justly. Dhārṣṭaka

was the son of Dhṛṣṭa; though he was a Kṣaṭtriya, he attained to Brāhmanaphood. He was naturally weak in fighting; always he was engaged in the works relating to the Brāhmanas.

गर्धातैतलनयक्षापूवतनों नाम विश्रुतः ।

सुकन्या च तथा पुत्री कप्लतावणयसंपुता ॥ 24 ॥

च्चवनाय सुता दत्ता राज्ञाधंाय सुन्दी ।

मुनि: सुतोच्नो जाततस्वा: शीतनुर्णेन ह् ॥ 25 ॥

विद्धितो रविपुत्राध्यामश्रिध्यात्मिन त श्रुतम् ।

Ānarta was the well known son and Sukanyā was the beautiful daughter of Saryāti. The King Saryāti gave his beautiful daughter in marriage to the blind Cyavana Rsi; but the Rsi, though blind, got his beautiful eyes again by the good character of the daughter. We heard that the Asvins, the Twins, the sons of the Sun, gave him back his eyesight.

जनमेव ज्ञवा

संदेहोर्ड महान्द्रकृ-काव्यायो कवित्तस्या ॥ 26 ॥

यज्ञात मुनेयेऊन्याद यदा युयी सुलोचना ।

कृकूपा गुणहीना वा नारी लक्षणवर्जिता ॥ 27 ॥

पुत्री यदा भवेत्रजा ततंधाय प्रवच्छति ।

जात्वार्धं सुमुखी कस्माहतवाव्युपसतमः ॥ 28 ॥

कारणं बृहि मे बहान्नुगाढोरित्य सर्वदा ।

Janamejaya said: O Brāhmaṇa! How is it that the King Śaryāti married his lovely-eyed daughter Sukanyā to the blind Cyavana Muni? I have got a great doubt on this point. The King gives his daughter in marriage to a blind person, if she be deformed, ill-qualified, or void of female signs. But the daughter, in this case was beautiful. How then Śaryāti, the Chief of Kings, gave over his daughter, knowing that the Rṣi was blind? O Brāhmaṇa! I am always an object of favour to you; so explain to me the cause of it.

सूत उवाच

इति राजा चकः शुत्वा परीक्षितसुनस्य वै ॥ 29 ॥

द्वैपायन: प्रसजात्या तमुवाच हरिसज्ञ ।

Sūta said: Glad to hear these words of Janamejaya, the Muni Dvaippāyna smilingly said:

Not very far off from the city, there was a beautiful lake of clear waters, like the Mānasasarovara lake. A Ghāt way (steps made of stones) descended into the lake.

Five varieties of lotuses were there in full bloom, bees were humming there all around. Various trees, Śala, Tāmāla, Sarala, Punnāgas, Aśokas, Banyans, Peepuls, Kadambas, rows of banana trees, Jambiras, dates, Panasas, betelnut tree, cocoanut trees, Ketakas, Kāñcanas, and other various beautiful trees encircled round the lake. Within

these, the white Yāthikās, Mallikās, and other creepers and shrubs were seen beautifying the scenery.

जंब्याप्रतिनिणीभिश्र करंजकुटजायुत् ।

पलाशनिवल्यदिविवल्यानलकमपिडत् ॥ 39 ॥

बभूव वोकिलावा: केकास्वनिवारजित्न् ।

तस्मारीय गुधे देरेगे पादपानां गणायुते ॥ 40 ॥

भागवंश्यवन: शान्ततावास: संश्विता मुनि: ।

Especially there were, amongst them, jack trees, mango trees, tamarind trees, Karanjas, Kutakas, Palāsas, Neem trees, Khadiras, Bel trees, and Āmalaki trees; and peacocks were sounding their notes, cuckoos were cooing their beautiful voices. Close to that place, there was, in a sacred grove covered over by trees, staying Cyavana Muni, the Bhrgu's son, of a tranquilled mind, and the chief of the ascetics.

जालाउसी विजर्भ स्थान तपसेने समाहितः ॥ 4 1 ॥

कृत्वा दुवासने मौनमाधाय जित्नातकतः ।

इन्द्रियाणि च संयम्य त्यक्ताहारतपोनिधि: ॥ 4 2 ॥

जलपानादिरितो ध्यायज्ञाने पराधिकम् ।

सवल्मीको3पवद्धजल्लताभि: परिवहितः ॥ 4 3 ॥

Thinking the place lovely and free from any obstacles, the Muni took his firm seat there and, collecting all his thought within himself, took the vow of non-speaking and controlling his breath became engaged in practising tapasyā. Restraining his senses and foregoing eating and drinking, that Muni constantly meditated on Bhagavatī of the nature of Sat, Cit and Ānanda. O King! While he was thus meditating, the anthill grew up round and covered his body and nice creepers covered that also all round.

कालेन महता राजनसामाकर्णी चिपीलिके:।

तथा स संवृतो धीमानुत्पण्ड इव सर्वतैः ॥ 44 ॥

कदाचित्म स्मीपालः कामिनीगणसंवृतः ।

आजगाम सरी राजन्वहतुमिदमुतामम् ॥ 45 ॥

O King! Long intervals passed away and it was covered over with ants; so much so that that intelligent Muni was covered entirely and looked

like a heap of earth. O King! Once the King Śaryāti wanted to play in an artificial wilderness and came there to the lake with his wives.

श्यापिति: सुन्दरीवृदंसयुतः सलिलेउमले ।

क्रीडासनो महीपालो बधूव कमलाकरे ॥ 46 ॥

सुकन्या वनमासाधा विजहार सखीवृता ।

सुमनांसि विधिन्यन्ती चझताना चझलोगम ॥ 47 ॥

Śaryāti became at once deeply engaged in playing on the clear waters of the lake, surrounded by the beautiful females. On the other hand, the quick beautiful daughter Sukanyā, picking up flowers here and there with her companions also began to play.

सर्वाभिरयासंयुक्ता राणचरणान्युपा ।

घंकमभागा वलकीच क्ववनस्य समादद्त् ॥ 48 ॥

Dressed in ornaments, Sukanyā walked to and fro; her anklets making a beautiful tinkling sound, till she came to the ant-hill of Cyavana Rsi.

क्रीडासभोपविष्ठा वरवीक्यास समीपतः ।

दर्शं गायस्य रथे वे ख्योते इव ज्योतिगे ॥ 49 ॥

किमेतादिति संचित्य समुद्रुं मनो दूधे ।

गृहीता कंटकं तीक्षा त्वसागण कृगोदरी ॥ 50 ॥

She sportingly sat close to that ant-hill and instantly saw a shining substance inside through that, like fireflies. “What is this?” She thought and wishing to take it, took a thorn and became very eager to prick it up.

सा बृद्धया मुनिना बाला समीपस्था कृतोद्धगा ।

विवारन्तो सुकेशान्ता मन्थध्येवेक कामिने ॥ 51 ॥

Slowly she went close to it and no sooner she got ready to prick it, than the Muni saw the beautiful, good-haired daughter as if to one's liking.

तां बीक्ष्य सुत्ती तत क्षामकण्ठसोपोर्दिति: ।

ताभाषावत कल्याणि किमेदोदिति भारणि : ॥ 52 ॥

The ascetic Bhārgava, seeing that auspicious nice lady with nice teeth, spoke out in a feeble voice: "What are you doing? O thin-bellied One! I am an ascetic; better go away from here.

दूरं गच्छ शिलालाक्ष तापसोर्शे घरानने ।

मा भिंदस्वाघ वर्षीक कणटकेन कृशोदिर ॥ 53 ॥

You have got such big-eyes, yet you do not see me. I therefore forbid you in your this attempt; do not pierce the anthill with a thorn.

तेनेदे प्रोष्ट्यमानापि सा धारस्य ने शुणोति वै ।

किमु खल्सिदमितुस्कृता निर्विधेत्यल लोचेन । ५४।।

Though prevented, the daughter could not hear his words and asking “what was that?” pierced his two-eyes with thorns.

दैवेन नोदिता भिन्चा जागम नृपकन्यका ।

क्रीड़नी शंकमाना स किं कृतं तु मवेति च ॥ 55 ॥

Thus impelled by Fate, the princess sportingly pierced his eyes; but she suspected and thought "What have I done?"

चुकीधि स तथा विद्वानेः परममन्युभान् ।

वेदनाध्वितिः कामं पतिरापं जगाम ह ॥ 56 ॥

गकृष्मूरनिरोधोमे भूलैमिकानां तु तक्षणात् ।

विशेषेण तु भपस्य सामानस्य समंततः ॥ 57 ॥

गजोष्ट्रतुगगाणां च सर्वेषां प्राणिनां तदा ।

ततो क्रूंदे गकृम्भुत्रे गर्शितिदुःवित्तोभवत् ॥ 58 ॥

Thus becoming afraid she returned from that spot. His two eyes being pricked, the great Muni exceedingly pained, became very wrathful; he incessantly gave vent to sorrows and remorse, being restless with pain. At that instant it happened that the king, ministers, soldiers, elephants, horses, camels, so much so that all the beings that were there, had all their evacuations (passing their urines and faeces) stopped. Seeing thus happened all on a sudden, the King Śaryāti was very much pained and became very anxious.

सैनिकः कथितं तस्ये शकुन्मूत्रनिरोधनम् ।

चिन्तयामास भूपालः कारणं दुःखसम्पदे ॥ 59 ॥

All the soldiers came to the King and informed him of the stoppage of their evacuations. The King thought over the cause why this had happened.

विश्विष्यह ततो राजा सैनिकान्यवजनासंखा ।

गृह्माग्य चिन्तातैः केनेदे दृष्टतु कृतम् ॥ 60 ॥

Cogitating thus, the King returned home. Becoming very much troubled with cares and anxieties, He asked his soldiers and kinsman “Who amongst you has done such an heinous act?”

On the west side of the lake the Maharsi Cyavana is practising the great tapasyā in the midst of the forest; I think some one has done mischief to that king of ascetics, blazing like a fire; and therefore we are overcome with this disease. The high-souled aged son of Bhrgu has become specially proficient in his asceticism and has become supreme; I think someone must have injured him.

ज्ञातं वा यदि वाज्ञातं तस्यैंद फलमुत्तमम् ।

कैक्षु दुहैः कृतः तस्य हेतनं तापस्यस्य  ॥64 ॥

Though I do not know who is that mischievous person that has shown him contempt or like that, this our state at present clearly shews that this is the fit punishment of that."

इति पृड़सामुक्ते सैनिका वेदनादिततः ।

मनोवाक्कायजितेन न विवोउपकृतं ववम् ॥ 65 ॥

इति श्रीमद्दैभीभागवते महापुरुषो सतमन्कन्धे

हितीयोउवायः 112 11

Hearing this, the soldiers said: None of us has committed any mischief by word, mind or body; we know this very well.

Here ends the Second Chapter of the Seventh Book on the piercing of the eyes of Cyavana
