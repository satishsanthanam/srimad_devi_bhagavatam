# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 7, CHAPTER 25

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 1</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ७.२५ पञ्चविंशोऽध्यायः । चाण्डालाज्ञया हरिश्चन्द्रस्य खड्गग्रहणवर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> सूत उवाच -
> एकदा तु गतो रन्तुं बालकैः सहितो बहिः ।
> वाराणस्या नातिदूरे रोहिताख्यः कुमारकः ॥ १ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Sūta said :-- Here, on the other hand, one day the boy Rohitāśva went out with other boys to play at some place close to Kāṣ’ī.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> क्रीडां कृत्वा ततो दर्भान् ग्रहीतुमुपचक्रमे ।
> कोमलानल्पमूलांश्च साग्राञ्छक्त्यनुसारतः ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He first played with the his comrades; he then began to root out and collect, as far as he could, the Darbha (Kuśa) grass, with its ends and which had not deep roots.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 3-4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> आर्यप्रीत्यर्थमित्युक्त्वा हस्तयुग्मेन यत्नतः ।
> सलक्षणाश्च समिधो बर्हिरिध्मं सलक्षणम् ॥ ३ ॥
> 
> पलाशकाष्ठान्यादाय त्वग्निहोमार्थमादरात् ।
> मस्तके भारकं कृत्वा खिद्यमानः पदे पदे ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On being questioned why he was taking the Dharba grass, Rohitā told his comrades that his master was a Brāhmin and that he was collecting them for his satisfaction. Saying this, he began to collect carefully by his hands the sacrificial fuel (Samidha) and other fuel for the burning purposes.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 5-6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> उदकस्थानमासाद्य तदा बालस्तृषान्वितः ।
> भुवि भारं विनिक्षिप्य जलस्थाने तदा शिशुः ॥ ५ ॥
> 
> कामतः सलिलं पीत्वा विश्रम्य च मुहूर्तकम् ।
> वल्मीकोपरि विन्यस्तभारो हर्तुं प्रचक्रमे ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He collected the Palāsa wood for Homa purpose and making it into a bundle with other articles already collected, took it on his head, but at every step he seemed to be fatigued. Feeling thirsty he went to a pool of water close by and keeping his load on the ground went down to drink water.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विश्वामित्राज्ञया तावत्कृष्णसर्पो भयावहः ।
> महाविषो महाघोरो वल्मीकान्निर्गतस्तदा ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Drinking water he rested a while and then as he had kept his load on the anthill, be began to take it back on his head, a very poisonous deadly serpent came out suddenly out of that anthill at the order of Viśvāmitra.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 8-9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तेनासौ बालको दष्टस्तदैव च पपात ह ।
> रोहिताख्यं मृतं दृष्ट्वा ययुर्बाला द्विजालयम् ॥ ८ ॥
> 
> त्वरिता भयसंविग्नाः प्रोचुस्तन्मातुरग्रतः ।
> हे विप्रदासि ते पुत्रः क्रीडां कर्तुं बहिर्गतः ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The snake immediately bit the boy who instantly fell down and died. His comrades seeing Rohitāśva dead went to the house of the Brāhmin. With much anxiety the boys went soon out of fear, to his mother and said :-- “O Brāhmin’s maidservant! Your son went out with us to play outside; but suddenly a poisonous snake bit him and he is dead.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 10-11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अस्माभिः सहितस्तत्र सर्पदष्टो मृतस्ततः ।
> इति सा तद्वचः श्रुत्वा वज्रपातोपमं तदा ॥ १० ॥
> 
> पपात मूर्च्छिता भूमौ छिन्नेव कदली यथा ।
> अथ तां ब्राह्मणो रुष्टः पानीयेनाभ्यषिञ्चत ॥ ११ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Rohitā’s mother, hearing these cruel words like thunder and lightning at once fell down on the ground like a plantain tree, cut off from its roots. The Brāhmin, then, came and sprinkled water on her face. When she regained her consciousness, the Brāhmīn then angrily spoke:

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 12-13</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मुहूर्ताच्चेतनां प्राप्ता ब्राह्मणस्तामथाब्रवीत् ।
> ब्राह्मण उवाच -
> अलक्ष्मीकारकं निन्द्यं जानती त्वं निशामुखे ॥ १२ ॥
> 
> रोदनं कुरुषे दुष्टे लज्जा ते हृदये न किम् ।
> ब्राह्मणेनैवमुक्ता सा न किञ्चिद्वाक्यमब्रवीत् ॥ १३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O wicked One! It is very inauspicious to cry at the evening time; especially the disfavour of the Goddess Lakṣmī; the poverty comes to the householder, you know this; why are you then weeping? Have you not a bit of shame in your heart? She made no reply at this.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> रुरोद करुणं दीना पुत्रशोकेन पीडिता ।
> अश्रुपूर्णमुखी दीना धूसरा मुक्तमूर्धजा ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Rather very much immersed in grief for her son, she wept in a pitiful voice. Her body was covered with dust, hairs were dishevelled and her face covered all over with tears from eyes. She constantly wept out of sorrow.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 15-16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अथ तां कुपितो विप्रो राजपत्नीमभाषत ।
> धिक्त्वां दुष्टे क्रयं गृह्य मम कार्यं विलुम्पसि ॥ १५ ॥
> 
> अशक्ता चेत्कथं तर्हि गृहीतं मम तद्धनम् ।
> एवं निर्भर्त्सिता तेन क्रूरवाक्यैः पुनः पुनः ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The Brāhmin, then, became very angry and spoke to the queen :-- “O Villain! O Wicked! Fie on you. I have bought you for money; yet you are hindering my luck. If you had this thought that you would not work under me, why did you take for nothing my money?”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 17-18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> रुदिता कारणं प्राह विप्रं गद्गदया गिरा ।
> स्वामिन् मम सुतो बालः सर्पदष्टो मृतो बहिः ॥ १७ ॥
> 
> अनुज्ञां मे प्रयच्छस्व द्रष्टुं यास्यामि बालकम् ।
> दुर्लभं दर्शनं तेन सञ्जातं मम सुव्रत ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Thus repeatedly scolded by the Brāhmin, she pitifully cried and spoke to the Brāhmin in a voice choked with feelings :-- “O Lord! My son has fallen into the jaws of death, being smitten by a serpent. O One of good vows! I will never be able to see him. So kindly permit me to go and see my boy.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> इत्युक्त्वा करुणं बाला पुनरेव रुरोद ह ।
> पुनस्तां कुपितो विप्रो राजपत्नीमभाषत ॥ १९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Saying thus, that lady began again to weep in a pitiful voice. The Brāhmin became very angry and spoke thus :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ब्राह्मण उवाच -
> शठे दुष्टसमाचारे किं न जानासि पातकम् ।
> यः स्वामिवेतनं गृह्य तस्य कार्यं विलुम्पति ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Cheat! Your conduct is extremely blameable; you do not know how one commits a sin. The man who taking his pay from his master spoils his master’s work, he goes to the terrible hell Raurava and is being scorched there.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 21-23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> नरके पच्यते सोऽथ महारौरवपूर्वके ।
> उषित्वा नरके कल्पं ततोऽसौ कुक्कुटो भवेत् ॥ २१ ॥
> 
> किमनेनाथवा कार्यं धर्मसङ्कीर्तनेन मे ।
> यस्तु पापरतो मूर्खः क्रूरो नीचोऽनृतः शठः ॥ २२ ॥
> 
> तद्वाक्यं निष्फलं तस्मिन्भवेद् बीजमिवोषरे ।
> एहि ते विद्यते किञ्चित्परलोकभयं यदि ॥ २३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Living in the Hell for a short while, he is born as a cock. Or it is useless for me to give you this instruction of the Dharma Śāstra, for to speak to such to an illiterate, cruel, low, hypocrite and liar and to one addicted to sinful acts is to sow seed on an usar land and to see it fruitless. Now if you have any fear for the afterlife, come and do the household affairs.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> एवमुक्ताथ सा विप्रं वेपमानाब्रवीद्वचः ।
> कारुण्यं कुरु मे नाथ प्रसीद सुमुखो भव ॥ २४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hearing this, she said to the Brāhmin, trembling :-- “O Lord! Be graciously pleased and shew your mercy on a maidservant.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 25-26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रस्थापय मुहूर्तं मां यावद् द्रक्ष्यामि बालकम् ।
> एवमुक्त्वाथ सा मूर्ध्ना निपत्य द्विजपादयोः ॥ २५ ॥
> 
> रुरोद करुणं बाला पुत्रशोकेन पीडिता ।
> अथाह कुपितो विप्रः क्रोधसंरक्तलोचनः ॥ २६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Only for a moment I will go to see the dead son of mine; so give me order to go there for a moment.” That lady was deeply absorbed with sorrows for her son; then she put her head on the feet of the Brāhmin and with a pitiful voice cried. The angry Brāhmin with eyes reddened then began to speak.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 27-28</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विप्र उवाच -
> किं ते पुत्रेण मे कार्यं गृहकर्म कुरुष्व मे ।
> किं न जानासि मे क्रोधं कशाघातफलप्रदम् ॥ २७ ॥
> 
> एवमुक्ता स्थिता धैर्याद् गृहकर्म चकार ह ।
> अर्धरात्रो गतस्तस्याः पादाभ्यङ्गादिकर्मणा ॥ २८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

What purpose of mine will be served by your son? Don’t you know about my anger? Have you forgotten about my whipping? So be ready and do my household work without any delay. Hearing his words, the queen held her patience and began to do the household work. She spent half the night time, when she finished champooing his feet.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 29-30</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ब्राह्मणेनाथ सा प्रोक्ता पुत्रपार्श्वं व्रजाधुना ।
> तस्य दाहादिकं कृत्वा पुनरागच्छ सत्वरम् ॥ २९ ॥
> 
> न लुप्येत यथा प्रातर्गृहकर्म ममेति च ।
> ततस्त्वेकाकिनी रात्रौ विलपन्ती जगाम ह ॥ ३० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When this was over, the Brāhmin spoke to her :-- “You can go now to your son; but see, finish his burning ceremonies and come back quickly. See that my morning works do not suffer.” Thus getting the permission, the Queen went at that dead of night to look for her son, alone and weeping.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 31-32</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> दृष्ट्वा मृतं निजं पुत्रं भृशं शोकेन पीडिता ।
> यूथभ्रष्टा कुरङ्गीव विवत्सा सौरभी यथा ॥ ३१ ॥
> 
> वाराणस्या बहिर्गत्वा क्षणाद् दृष्ट्वा निजं सुतम् ।
> शयानं रङ्कवद्भूमौ काष्ठदर्भतृणोपरि ॥ ३२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Gradually she went out of the precincts of the city of Kāṣī and there she saw her son like a poor man’s son lying on the ground over leaves and pieces of woods. Seeing her son dead, the humble Queen was very troubled with sorrow like an antelope, straying from its herd and as a cow missing her calf.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 33-34</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विललापातिदुःखार्ता शब्दं कृत्वा सुनिष्ठुरम् ।
> एहि मे सम्मुखं कस्माद्रोषितोऽसि वदाधुना ॥ ३३ ॥
> 
> आयास्यभिमुखो नित्यमम्बेत्युक्त्वा पुनः पुनः ।
> गत्वा स्खलत्पदा तस्य पपातोपरि मूर्छिता ॥ ३४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The Queen Mādhavī then began to lament, in a very pitiful tone, thus :-- “O my Son! Come once before me; say why you are angry. Oh! My child! You used to come frequently to me, uttering Ma! Ma! Then why are you not coming now?”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 35-36</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पुनः सा चेतनां प्राप्य दोर्भ्यामालिङ्ग्य बालकम् ।
> तन्मुखे वदनं न्यस्य रुरोदार्तस्वनैस्तदा ॥ ३५ ॥
> 
> कराभ्यां ताडनं चक्रे मस्तकस्योदरस्य च ।
> हा बाल हा शिशो वत्स हा कुमारक सुन्दर ॥ ३६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Saying thus, she tumbling went and fell over his son. She, regaining her consciousness, embraced her son and placing her face on the face of the child began to weep pitifully. “Oh! My son! Oh! My child! Oh my Kumāra! Oh! My Beautiful! and began to beat her head and her breast with her hands.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 37-38</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हा राजन् क्व गतोऽसि त्वं पश्येमं बालकं निजम् ।
> प्राणेभ्योऽपि गरीयांसं भूतले पतितं मृतम् ॥ ३७ ॥
> 
> तथापश्यन्मुखं तस्य भूयो जीवितशङ्कया ।
> निर्जीववदनं ज्ञात्वा मूर्छिता निपपात ह ॥ ३८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O King! Where are you now? You used to look upon your son dearer than even your life. Your that son is now lying dead on the ground. Come and behold him once. It seems that the son has got back his life.” Thus thinking she looked upon his face; but when it looked dead, she fell immediately unconscious.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 39-40</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हस्तेन वदनं गृह्य पुनरेवमभाषत ।
> शयनं त्यज हे बाल शीघ्रं जागृहि भीषणम् ॥ ३९ ॥
> 
> निशार्धं वर्धते चेदं शिवाशतनिनादितम् ।
> भूतप्रेतपिशाचादिडाकिनीयूथनादितम् ॥ ४० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Getting back soon her consciousness, she held his face by her hands and said :-- “O Child! Rise up from your sleep; awake; now is the dreadful night time; hundreds of jackals are yelling into our ears. Even Pretas, Bhutas, Piśācas and Dākinīs are roaming in packs and making terrible sounds Hum, Hum. Your comrades returned to their homes just at sunset; Why are you alone remaining here?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 41-42</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मित्राणि ते गतान्यस्तात्त्वमेकस्तु कुतः स्थितः ।
> सूत उवाच -
> एवमुक्त्वा पुनस्तन्वी करुणं प्ररुरोद ह ॥ ४१ ॥
> 
> हा शिशो बाल हा वत्स रोहिताख्य कुमारक ।
> रे पुत्र प्रतिशब्दं मे कस्मात्त्वं न प्रयच्छसि ॥ ४२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Sūta said :-- The thin-bodied queen, thus saying, began to lament, “Oh my Child! Oh! My son, Oh! Rohitāśva, O Kumāra, why are you not replying to my words!

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 43</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तवाहं जननी वत्स किं न जानासि पश्य माम् ।
> देशत्यागाद्राज्यनाशात्पुत्र भर्त्रा स्वविक्रयात् ॥ ४३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Oh my Child! I am your mother; do you not recognise me; look at me once. O Child! I am deprived of my kingdom and exiled from my country; my husband has sold even his body and I am myself reduced to slavery.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 44</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> यद्दासीत्वाच्च जीवामि त्वां दृष्ट्वा पुत्र केवलम् ।
> ते जन्मसमये विप्रैरादिष्टं यत्त्वनागतम् ॥ ४४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

What man is there that can live in this state! I am living simply by seeing your lotus-face. The astrologer who cast your horoscope at your birth, calculated future events in your life; but where? none of them is fructified. They said that this child will be a hero, warrior, long-lived, very charitable man, and always ready to do the worship of the Devas, Dvījas and the Gurus.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 25 Verse 45-47</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> दीर्घायुः पृथिवीराजः पुत्रपौत्रसमन्वितः ।

**English Translation:**

You are a servant and what you have spoken is not fit for a servant. Working as a slave of a Cāṇḍāla, you are speaking the words of the gods. Therefore, O slave! hear now what I say; no need of exchanging any further words. O Shameless One! If you fear sin a bit why then did you accept the slavery in a Cāṇḍāla’s house. Take this sword and cut off her head.” Thus speaking the Cāṇḍāla gave him the axe. Here ends the Twenty-fifth Chapter of the Seventh Book on the quarrels between Hariścandra and Viśvāmitra in the Mahā Purāṇam, Śrī Mad Devī Bhāgavatam, of 18,000 verses, by Maharṣi Veda Vyāsa.
