# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 7, CHAPTER 9

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 1-2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ७.९ नवमोऽध्यायः । मान्धातोत्पत्तिवर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच -
> कदाचिदष्टकाश्राद्धे विकुक्षिं पृथिवीपतिः ।
> आज्ञापयदसम्मूढो मांसमानय सत्वरम् ॥ १ ॥
> 
> मेध्यं श्राद्धार्थमधुना वने गत्वा सूतादरात् ।
> उत्युक्तोऽसौ तथेत्याशु जगाम वनमस्त्रभृत् ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! Once on a time, the time for Aṣṭaka Śrāddha (the funeral ceremony in honour of the departed) arrived. Seeing this, the King Ikṣāku ordered his son Vikukṣi :-- “O Child! Go immediately to the forest and bring carefully pure sanctified meat for the Śrāddha purposes; see, that there be no neglect of duty.” Thus ordered, Vikukṣi instantly went to the forest equipped with arms.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 3-4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> गत्वा जघान बाणैः स वराहान्सूकरान्मृगान् ।
> शशांश्चापि परिश्रान्तो बभूवाथ बुभूक्षितः ॥ ३ ॥
> 
> विस्मृता चाष्टका तस्य शशं चाददसौ वने ।
> शेषं निवेदयामास पित्रे मांसमनुत्तमम् ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He hunted in the forest lots of boars, pigs, deer, and hare. But he was so very tired with his journey in the forest and got so hungry that he forgot everything about the Aṣṭaka Śrāddha and ate one hare there in the forest. The remaining excellent meat he brought and handed over to his father.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रोक्षणाय समानीतं मांसं दृष्ट्वा गुरुस्तदा ।
> अनर्हमिति तज्ज्ञात्वा चुकोप मुनिसत्तमः ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When that meat was brought to be sprinkled for purification, the family priest Vaśiṣṭha, on seeing it, at once came to know that some portion had already been eaten and it was the remaining part.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> भुक्तशेषं तु न श्राद्धे प्रोक्षणीयमिति स्थितिः ।
> राज्ञे निवेदयामास वसिष्ठः पाकदूषणम् ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The leavings of food are not fit for the sprinkling purposes; this is the Śāstric rule. Vaśiṣṭha informed the King of this defect in the food.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पुत्रस्य कर्म तज्ज्ञात्वा भूपतिर्गुरुणोदितम् ।
> चुकोप विधिलोपात्तं देशान्निःसारयत्ततः ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

In accordance with the Guru’s advice, the King coming to know thus the violation of the rule by his son, became very angry and banished his son from his kingdom.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 8</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शशाप इति विख्यातो नाम्ना जातो नृपात्मजः ।
> गतो वने शशादस्तु पितृकोपादसम्भ्रमः ॥ ८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The prince became known from that time as Sa’sāda; he did not become the least sorry for his father’s anger; he went to the forest and gladly remained there.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वन्येन वर्तयन्कालं नीतवान् धर्मतत्परः ।
> पितर्युपरते राज्यं प्राप्तं तेन महात्मना ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

He gladly passed his time absorbed in religion and sustained his life on forest fruits and roots. After sometime when his father died, he inherited his kingdom.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 10-11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शशादस्त्वकरोद्राज्यमयोध्यायाः पतिः स्वयम् ।
> यज्ञाननेकशः पूर्णांश्चकार सरयूतटे ॥ १० ॥
> 
> शशादस्याभवत्पुत्रः ककुत्स्थ इति विश्रुतः ।
> तस्यैव नामभेदाद्वै इन्द्रवाहः पुरञ्जयः ॥ ११ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On becoming the King of Ayodhyā, Śaśāda had only one son; he became famous in the three lokas by the name of Kakutstha. He was known also by other names Indravāha and Purañjaya.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 12</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> जनमेजय उवाच -
> नामभेदः कथं जातो राजपुत्रस्य चानघ ।
> कारणं ब्रूहि मे सर्वं कर्मणा येन चाभवत् ॥ १२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Janamejaya said :-- “O Holy One! How and why was the prince named Kakutstha. Why was he known by the two other names? Speak all this to me.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 13-14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> व्यास उवाच -
> शशादे स्वर्गते राजा ककुत्स्थ इति चाभवत् ।
> 
> एतस्मिन्नन्तरे देवा दैत्यैः सर्वे पराजिताः ॥ १३ ॥
> 
> जग्मुस्त्रिलोकाधिपतिं विष्णुं शरणमव्ययम् ।
> तान्प्रोवाच महाविष्णुस्तदा देवान्सनातनः ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! When Śaśāda went to the Heavens Kakutstha became king. That religious king then began to govern the country of his father and grandfather with an authority supported by a powerful arm. At this time the Devas suffered a defeat from the Dānavas and took refuge to Viṣṇu, the Infallible and the Lord of the three worlds. The eternal great Viṣṇu full of intelligence and bliss then addressed the Devas :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 15-16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विष्णुरुवाच -
> पार्ष्णिग्राहं महीपालं प्रार्थयन्तु शशादजम् ।
> स हनिष्यति वै दैत्यान्सङ्ग्रामे सुरसत्तमाः ॥ १५ ॥
> 
> आगमिष्यति धर्मात्मा साहाय्यार्थं धनुर्धरः ।
> पराशक्तेः प्रसादेन सामर्थ्यं तस्य चातुलम् ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Viṣṇu said :-- “O Devas! Go and pray to the King Śaśāda. He will be your ally and kill all the Demons. That King is religious; especially he is a worshipper of the Highest Śakti. He is a good archer and will come to help you. His strength is immense.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 17-18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हरेः सुवचनाद्देवा ययुः सर्वे सवासवाः ।
> अयोध्यायां महाराज शशादतनयं प्रति ॥ १७ ॥
> 
> तानागतान् सुरान् राजा पूजयामास धर्मतः ।
> पप्रच्छागमने राजा प्रयोजनमतन्द्रितः ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! Indra and the other Devas hearing the nectar like words of Hari went to Ayodhyā, to Kakutstha, the son of Śaśāda. Seeing the Devas at his palace, the king worshipped them duly and with great care and he asked them why they had come there.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 19-20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> राजोवाच -
> धन्योऽहं पावितश्चास्मि जीवितं सफलं मम ।
> यदागत्य गृहे देवा ददुश्च दर्शनं महत् ॥ १९ ॥
> 
> ब्रुवन्तु कृत्यं देवेशा दुःसाध्यमपि मानवैः ।
> करिष्यामि महत्कार्यं सर्वथा भवतां महत् ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King said :-- “O Devas! When you have favoured me by your presence here, I am blessed and sanctified; my life is crowned with success. Say what I can do for you; I will carry it out even if it be very hard for me to perform.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 21-22</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> देवा ऊचुः -
> साहाय्यं कुरु राजेन्द्र सखा भव शचीपतेः ।
> सङ्ग्रामे जय दैत्येन्द्रान्दुर्जयांस्त्रिदशैरपि ॥ २१ ॥
> 
> पराशक्तिप्रसादेन दुर्लभं नास्ति ते क्वचित् ।

>
**English Translation:**

The Devas said :-- “O Prince! Please help and back us and defeat the Daityas, invincible by the Devas and form an alliance with Indra. O King! By the grace of the Highest Śakti, you have nothing unattained anywhere; so we have come to you by the order of Viṣṇu.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

**English Translation:**

The King said :-- “O Devas! I can back you and become your ally if Indra carries me on his back in the time of war.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

I will fight now with the Daityas for the Devas; but I will go to the battle-field on Indra’s back; this I speak to you truly.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 25</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! The Devas then spoke to Indra :-- “O Lord of Śaci! It is now your bounden duty to do this; so quitting shame, be a carrier to this King.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

Indra got ashamed very much, but being requested frequently by Hari, at last assumed the appearance of a bull like the great Bull of Śiva.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तमारुरोह राजासौ सङ्ग्रामगमनाय वै ।
> स्थितः ककुदि येनास्य ककुत्स्थस्तेन चाभवत् ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King mounted on that bull to go to the war; he fought while taking his seat on the hump on the shoulders of the bull (Kakud); therefore he was named Kakutstha.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 28-29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> इन्द्रो वाहः कृतो येन तेन नाम्नेन्द्रवाहकः ।
> पुरं जितं तु दैत्यानां तेनाभूच्च पुरञ्जयः ॥ २८ ॥
> 
> जित्वा दैत्यान्महाबाहुर्धनं तेषां प्रदत्तवान् ।
> पप्रच्छ चैवं राजर्षेरिति सख्यं बभूव ह ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The King was carried by Indra on his back hence he was named Indravāha; he conquered the Dānavas in battle; hence he was called Purañjaya. The powerful King defeated the Dānavas and gave away all their wealth to the Devas. He bade farewell to the Devas and returned to his own kingdom. Thus the alliance was formed with Indra.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

O King! Kakutstha became very celebrated on this earth; his descendants became kings and were known as Kakutsthas and were all very famous here

</details>

<details>

Kakutstha had one powerful son, named Kākutstha by his legal wife; Kākutstha had the son Prithu, of mighty prowess.

<details>

>
**English Translation:**

Prithu was the part incarnation personified of Viṣṇu, and worshipper of the feet of the Supreme Śakti. His son was Visvarandhi; he became king and governed the kingdom.

</details>

<details>

His son was Candra; he came to be king, governed his subjects and multiplied very much his issues.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 34</b></summary>

**English Translation:**

Yuvanāśva was one of his sons; he was very powerful and spirited. Śavanta was the son of Yuvanāśva; he was very religious. He built a nice city named Śāvantī like the Paradise of Indra.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 9 Verse 35-36</b></summary>

Brihadaśva was the son of the high-souled Śāvanta; he had a son Kuvalayāśva. He became the Lord of the earth by the power of his arms. He killed Dhundu Dānava; so he was very much celebrated by the name of Dhundumāra.

</details>

<details>

His son was Dridhāśva; he governed the earth; His son was Śrīmān Haryaśva. His son was Nikumbha; he became the King. Nikumbha had his son Varhaṇāśva. Kriśāśva was his son.

</details>

His son was the powerful Parasenajit; his son’s prowess knew no bounds. Parasenajit had the fortunate son Yauvanāśva.

</details>

O fortunate One! The son of Yauvanaśva was Śrīmān Māndhātā; he became the Lord of the Earth and for the satisfaction of the Devī Bhagavatī had one thousand and eight palaces built in Benares and in the other chief places of pilgrimages.

Māudhātā was not born of his mother’s womb but was born in the belly of his father. Then the ministers tore asunder the belly of his father and got him out.

<details>

Janamejaya said :-- O fortunate One! What you said was never seen nor heard ever before since. This sort of birth is highly improbable. How was that beautiful son born in the belly of his father? Describe this in detail and satisfy my curiosity.

</details>

**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! The King Yauvanāśva had one hundred queens; yet he had no issues. The King always thought much about his son. Once the King, sorry and desirous of a son, went to the holy retreats of the Ṛṣis. On arriving there, he began frequently to respire heavily before the ascetics. The Ṛṣis became filled with pity on seeing his sorrowful condition. O King! The Brāhmins that said to him :-- O King! Why are you thus sorrowful and distressed? What is your sorrow that is troubling your heart? Speak truly. We will surely redress your grievance.
