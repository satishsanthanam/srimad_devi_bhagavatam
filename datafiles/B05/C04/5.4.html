Ch 4 Verse 1-2 Verse
Sanskrit Text
५.४ चतुर्थोऽध्यायः । भयातुरेन्द्रादिदेवैः सुरगुरुणा सह परामर्शवर्णनम् । व्यास उवाच । गते दूते सुरेन्द्रोऽपि समाहूय सुरानथ । यमवायुधनाध्यक्षवरुणानिदमूचिवान् ॥ १ ॥ महिषो नाम दैत्येन्द्रो रम्भपुत्रो महाबलः । वरदर्पमदोन्मत्तो मायाशतविचक्षणः ॥ २ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- O King! The messenger of the Dānavas having departed, Indra, the lord of the Devas, Yama, Vāyu, Varuṇa, and Kuvera and other Devas, called an assembly and addressed thus :-- O Devas! the most powerful Mahiṣa, the son of Rambha, is now the king of the Dānavas; he is particularly expert in hundreds of Māyās (magic) and has become haughty on the strength of his boon.

English Translation
O Devas! Mahiṣa has sent his messenger; he wants to take possession of the heaven; he came down to me and spoke thus :--
Ch 4 Verse 4 Verse
Sanskrit Text
त्यज देवालयं शक्र यथेच्छं व्रज वासव । सेवां वा कुरु दैत्यस्य महिषस्य महात्मनः ॥ ४ ॥
English Translation
“O Indra! Quit your this heaven and go any where you like, or be ready to pay your homage to the highsouled Mahiṣāsura, the Lord of the Dānavas.
Ch 4 Verse 5 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दयावान्दानवेन्द्रोऽसौ स ते वृत्तिं विधास्यति । नतेषु भृत्यभूतेषु न कुप्यति कदाचन ॥ ५ ॥
English Translation
The Dānava Chief never becomes angry with his opponent who becomes submissive like a servant; if you surrender and serve him, he will, out of mercy, grant an allowance to you.
Ch 4 Verse 6 Verse
Sanskrit Text
नोचेद्युद्धाय देवेश सेनोद्योगं कुरु स्वयम् । गते मयि स दैत्येन्द्रस्त्वरितः समुपेष्यति ॥ ६ ॥
English Translation
O Lord of the Devas! If this does not like you, then collect your forces and be ready for fight; no sooner I return, the Lord of the Dānavas will come here at once ready to give battle to you.”
Ch 4 Verse 7 Verse
Sanskrit Text
इत्युक्त्वा स गतो दूतो दानवस्य दुरात्मनः । किं कर्तव्यमतः कार्यं चिन्तयध्वं सुरोत्तमाः ॥ ७ ॥
English Translation
Thus saying, the messenger of that wicked Dānava departed. Now what are we to do? O Devas! Think on that.
Ch 4 Verse 8 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दुर्बलोऽपि न चोपेक्ष्यः शत्रुर्बलवता सुराः । विशेषेण सदोद्योगी बलवान्बलदर्पितः ॥ ८ ॥
English Translation
O Devas! Even a weak enemy is not to be overlooked by a powerful opponent, especially when the enemy is powerful by his own powers and is ever energetic, never is he to be overlooked.
Ch 4 Verse 9 Verse
Sanskrit Text
उद्यमः किल कर्तव्यो यथाबुद्धि यथाबलम् । दैवाधीनो भवेन्नूनं जयो वाथ पराजयः ॥ ९ ॥
English Translation
It is always iñcumbent on us to make our efforts, as best as we can, both by our body and mind as far as lies in our power; the result, victory or defeat depends entirely on Fate.
Ch 4 Verse 10 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सन्धियोगो न चात्रास्ति खले सन्धिर्निरर्थकः । सर्वथा साधुभिः कार्यं विचार्य च पुनः पुनः ॥ १० ॥
English Translation
It is useless to make treaty with a deceitful and dishonest person; we therefore never should make treaty with this person; you are all honest; that Dānava is dishonest; therefore ponder and ponder deeply and ponder again; do you that which is proper.
Ch 4 Verse 11 Verse
Sanskrit Text
यानमप्यधुना नैव कर्तव्यं सहसा पुनः । प्रेक्षकाः प्रेषणीयाश्च शीघ्रगाः सुप्रवेशकाः ॥ ११ ॥
English Translation
It is not advisable to go out at once for fight when we are unaware of our enemy’s strength; let us therefore send spies truthful, honest, motiveless, quick, to ascertain their strength, those who can easily enter amongst our enemies and yet who have no relation, nor any interest with them.
Ch 4 Verse 12 Verse
Sanskrit Text
इङ्गितज्ञाश्च निःसङ्गा निःस्पृहाः सत्यवादिनः । सेनाभियोगं प्रस्थानं बलसङ्ख्यां यथार्थतः ॥ १२ ॥
English Translation
The arrangements of their forces, their movements, their numbers, they will ascertain correctly who are their generals, what is their number and what is their strength, they will thoroughly examine and return here quickly.
Ch 4 Verse 13 Verse
Sanskrit Text
वीराणां च परिज्ञानं कृत्वायान्तु त्वरान्विताः । ज्ञात्वा दैत्यपतेस्तस्य सैन्यस्य च बलाबलम् ॥ १३ ॥
English Translation
First, we will ascertain the strength of the forces of our opposite party and then we will decide at once whether we will start for battle or seek protection within forts.
Ch 4 Verse 14 Verse
Sanskrit Text
करिष्यामि ततस्तूर्णं यानं वा दुर्गसङ्ग्रहम् । विचार्य खलु कर्तव्यं कार्यं बुद्धिमता सदा । सहसा विहितं कार्यं दुःखदं सर्वथा भवेत् ॥ १४ ॥
English Translation
Wise persons always consider before they act; any act done rashly leads in all respects to many troubles, and anything done after mature prejudgments leads to happiness; so the wise do.
Ch 4 Verse 15 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तस्माद्विमृश्य कर्तव्यं सुखदं सर्वथा बुधैः । नात्र भेदविधिर्न्याय्यो दानवेषु च सर्वथा ॥ १५ ॥
English Translation
The Dānavas are all one in their heart and mind; therefore it is not advisable, in any way to apply the principle of Bheda (sowing principles of discord).
Ch 4 Verse 16 Verse
Sanskrit Text
एकचित्तेषु कार्येऽस्मिंस्तस्माच्चारा व्रजन्तु वै । ज्ञात्वा बलाबलं तेषां पश्चान्नीतिर्विचार्य च ॥ १६ ॥
English Translation
Let our spies go there, ascertain their strength, return and inform us; we will then judge what principle is proper and apply to the expert Dānavas.
Ch 4 Verse 17 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विधेया विधिवत्तज्ज्ञैस्तेषु कार्यपरेषु च । अन्यथा विहितं कार्यं विपरीतफलप्रदम् ॥ १७ ॥
English Translation
Any act done contrary to policy and expediency will undoubtedly produce effects contrary in every way just like a medicine which we have not tried already.
Ch 4 Verse 18-19 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सर्वथा तद्भवेन्नूनमज्ञातमौषधं यथा । व्यास उवाच । इति सञ्चिन्त्य तैः सर्वैः प्रणिधिं कार्यवेदिनम् ॥ १८ ॥ प्रेषयामास देवेन्द्रः परिज्ञानाय पार्थिव । दूतस्तु त्वरितो गत्वा समागम्य सुराधिपम् ॥ १९ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- O King! Thus counselling with the Devas; Indra sent expert spies to ascertain the true state of affairs. The spies, too, went into the abode of the Daityas, with no delay and made their searches thoroughly into every nook and corner and returned and told Indra all the strength of the Dānava forces.
Ch 4 Verse 20-21 Verse
Sanskrit Text
निवेदयामास तदा सर्वसैन्यबलाबलम् । ज्ञात्वा तद्बलमुद्योगं तुराषाडतिविस्मितः ॥ २० ॥ देवानचोदयत्तूर्णं समाहूय पुरोहितम् । मन्त्रं मन्त्रविदां श्रेष्ठं चकार त्रिदशेश्वरः ॥ २१ ॥
English Translation
Indra was very much startled to know, then, of their arrangements. He immediately bade all the Devas be ready for battle and called for his High priest Brihaspati, expert in giving advices and began to consult with him how to carry on the warfare with that indomitable enemy, the Lord of the Asuras. On Bhihaspati, the best and famous of the Aṅgirā family, taking his excellent seat.
Ch 4 Verse 22 Verse
Sanskrit Text
उवाचाङ्गिरसश्रेष्ठं समासीनं वरासने । इन्द्र उवाच । भो भो देवगुरो विद्वन्किं कर्तव्यं वदस्व नः ॥ २२ ॥
English Translation
Indra thus said :-- “O Guru of the Devas! O Learned! Please say what are we to do now in this critical juncture? You are omniscient; to-day you are our guide.
Ch 4 Verse 23 Verse

सर्वज्ञोऽसि समुत्पन्ने कार्ये त्वं गतिरद्य नः । दानवो महिषो नाम महावीर्यो मदान्वितः ॥ २३ ॥
English Translation
The Demon Mahīṣa has become very powerful, very haughty; surrounded by Dānavas he is now coming to fight with us.
Ch 4 Verse 24 Verse
Sanskrit Text
योद्धुकामः समायाति बहुभिर्दानवैर्वृतः । तत्र प्रतिक्रिया कार्या त्वया मन्त्रविदाधुना ॥ २४ ॥
English Translation
You are expert in mantras; find out the remedy for us. Śukrācārya is the remover of all obstacles on their side; and that you are our safe guard is well known to us.”
Ch 4 Verse 25 Verse

तेषां शुक्रस्तथा त्वं मे विघ्नहर्ता सुसंयतः । व्यास उवाच । तच्छ्रुत्वा वचनं प्राह तुरासाहं बृहस्पतिः ॥ २५ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- Hearing these words of Indra, Brihaspati, who is always ready to effect the Deva’s purposes, thought intently on the subject, said very shortly thus :--
Ch 4 Verse 26 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विचिन्त्य मनसा कामं कार्यसाधनतत्परः । गुरुरुवाच । स्वस्थो भव सुरेन्द्र त्वं धैर्यमालम्ब्य मारिष ॥ २६ ॥
English Translation
Brihaspati spoke :-- O Lord of the Devas! O Venerable One! Be peaceful; have patience; when a difficulty comes, one should not, all on a sudden, lose one’s patience.
Ch 4 Verse 27 Verse

व्यसने च समुत्पन्ने न त्याज्यं धैर्यमाशु वै । जयाजयौ सुराध्यक्ष दैवाधीनौ सदैव हि ॥ २७ ॥
English Translation
O Chief of the Immortals! Victory or defeat is completely under the control of destiny: therefore intelligent ones should always be patient.
Ch 4 Verse 28 Verse
Sanskrit Text
स्थातव्यं धैर्यमालम्ब्य तस्माद् बुद्धिमता सदा । भवितव्यं भवत्येव जानन्नेव शतक्रतो ॥ २८ ॥
English Translation
O Śatakratu! What will unavoidably be done must come to pass; knowing this as certain, one would always be an enthusiast and exert one’s powers.
Ch 4 Verse 29-32 Verse
Sanskrit Text
उद्यमः सर्वथा कार्यो यथापौरुषमात्मनः । मुनयोऽपि हि मुक्त्यर्थमुद्यमैकरताः सदा ॥ २९ ॥ दैवाधीनं च जानन्तो योगध्यानपरायणाः । तस्मात्सदैव कर्तव्यो व्यवहारोदितोद्यमः ॥ ३० ॥ सुखं भवतु वा मा वा दैवे का परिदेवना । विना पुरुषकारेण कदाचित्सिद्धिमाप्नुयात् ॥ ३१ ॥ अन्धवत्पङ्गुवत्कामं न तथा मुदमावहेत् । कृते पुरुषकारेऽपि यदि सिद्धिर्न जायते ॥ ३२ ॥
English Translation
Everything is guided by Fate. Knowing this, the Munis devote themselves at all times solely filled with energy in their meditation and Yoga practices for their final liberation. Therefore, to show one’s energy, according to the rules of the daily practices, ought to be indispensably done; and one should not repel or feel pleasure on failure or success; for that is under Fate. Success sometimes comes without the exercise of one’s own powers, as seen in cases of the lame and the blind; and that is not the reason why one should be very glad.
Ch 4 Verse 33 Verse
Sanskrit Text
न तत्र दूषणं तस्य दैवाधीने शरीरिणि । कार्यसिद्धिर्न सैन्येऽस्ति न मन्त्रे न च मन्त्रणे ॥ ३३ ॥
English Translation
The embodied beings are all under Daiva (Fate); therefore even if success be not attained, though one’s own powers are exercised thoroughly, no one is to blame for that. O Lord of the Suras! What to say of forces, Mantras, or advices, what of chariots or weapons, nothing to lead to success; It is Daiva, and only Daiva that makes one successful.
Ch 4 Verse 34-36 Verse
Sanskrit Text
न रथे नायुधे नूनं दैवाधीना सुराधिप । बलवाङ्क्लेशमाप्नोति निर्बलः सुखमश्नुते ॥ ३४ ॥ बुद्धिमान्क्षुधितः शेते निर्बुद्धिर्भोगवान्भवेत् । कातरो जयमाप्नोति शूरो याति पराजयम् ॥ ३५ ॥ दैवाधीने तु संसारे कामं का परिदेवना । उद्यमे योजयेन्नूनं भवितव्यं सुराधिप ॥ ३६ ॥
English Translation
This whole universe is under Daiva; it is, therefore, that we see powerful persons suffering pains, and weak ones getting happiness; the intelligent ones sleeping without any food and fools enjoying merrily; distressed persons getting victory and powerful ones suffering defeats; what cares, then ought one to entertain in this. O Lord of the Suras! Whatever is inevitable to come to pass, be it success or failure, one will lead one’s energies to that end; therefore one needs to consider beforehand whether one’s energies will be successful or not. In times of distress, one sees distress too much and in times of pleasure, one seeks pleasure too much; one’s self, therefore one should not surrender to one’s enemies, pleasure and pain.
Ch 4 Verse 37-41 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दुःखदे सुखदे वापि तत्र तौ न विचिन्तयेत् । दुःखे दुःखाधिकान्पश्येत्सुखे पश्येत्सुखाधिकम् ॥ ३७ ॥ आत्मानं हर्षशोकाभ्यां शत्रुभ्यामिव नार्पयेत् । धैर्यमेवावगन्तव्यं हर्षशोकोद्भवे बुधैः ॥ ३८ ॥ अधैर्याद्यादृशं दुःखं न तु धैर्येऽस्ति तादृशम् । दुर्लभं सहनत्वं वै समये सुखदुःखयोः ॥ ३९ ॥ हर्षशोकोद्भवो यत्र न भवेद् बुद्धिनिश्चयात् । किं दुःखं कस्य वा दुःखं निर्गुणोऽहं सदाव्ययः ॥ ४० ॥ चतुर्विंशातिरिक्तोऽस्मि किं मे दुःखं सुखं च किम् । प्राणस्य क्षुत्पिपासे द्वे मनसः शोकमूर्छने ॥ ४१ ॥
English Translation
Pain and suffering is not felt so much in patience as is felt when impatient; therefore one must practise patience when pain or pleasure comes. Indeed it is very difficult to bear oneself up in distress or happiness; therefore wise persons try not to let these feelings crop up at all from the very beginning. “I am always full, undiminishable, I am beyond these Prākritic qualities. Who is there to suffer? What is suffering?” Thus one ought to think at that moment. I am beyond the twenty-four Tattvas; what pleasure or pain can, then, arise to me? Hunger and thirst are the Dharma of Prāṇa; pain and insensibility is the Dharma of mind, age and death belong to this physical body. I am free from these six diseases; I am Śiva.
Ch 4 Verse 42-44 Verse
Sanskrit Text
जरामृत्यू शरीरस्य षडूर्मिरहितः शिवः । शोकमोहौ शरीरस्य गणौ किं मेऽत्र चिन्तने ॥ ४२ ॥ शरीरं नाहमथवा तत्सम्बन्धी न चाप्यहम् । सप्तैकषोडशादिभ्यो विभिन्नोऽहं सदा सुखी ॥ ४३ ॥ प्रकृतिर्विकृतिर्नाहं किं मे दुःखं सदा पुनः । इति मत्वा सुरेश त्वं मनसा भव निर्ममः ॥ ४४ ॥
English Translation
Grief and delusion are the qualities of this body what then do I care for them? “I” am not the qualities of the body nor “I” am the soul pertaining to that. I am beyond the seven transfigurations, changes, e. g., Mahat, etc., I am beyond this Prakriti, Nature, and beyond the sixteen changes wrought out by Prakriti; I am therefore eternally happy, I am beyond Prakriti and its transformation, then why am I to suffer pain always? O Lord of the Suras! Think on these and be without any passion.
Ch 4 Verse 45-47 Verse
Sanskrit Text
उपायः प्रथमोऽयं ते दुःखनाशे शतक्रतो । ममता परमं दुःखं निर्ममत्वं परं सुखम् ॥ ४५ ॥ सन्तोषादपरं नास्ति सुखस्थानं शचीपते । अथवा यदि न ज्ञानं ममत्वनाशने किल ॥ ४६ ॥ ततो विवेकः कर्तव्यो भवितव्ये सुराधिप । प्रारब्धकर्मणां नाशो नाभोगाल्लक्ष्यते किल ॥ ४७ ॥
English Translation
O Śatakratu! This attachment is the root of all miseries and non-attachment is the source of all happiness; non-attachment therefore, is the chief means of the extirpation of all your troubles. Lord of Śaci! Nothing can be happier than contentment. In case you find it difficult to practise dispassion, apply, then, discrimination and think of Fate, that what comes inevitably to pass. O Lord of the Suras! Actions already done cannot die out without their effects being enjoyed.
Ch 4 Verse 48 Verse
Sanskrit Text
यद्भावि तद्भवत्येव का चिन्ता सुखदुःखयोः । सुरैः सर्वैः सहायैर्वा बुद्ध्या वा तव सत्तम ॥ ४८ ॥
English Translation
O Best of the Suras! Let all your intelligence be brought to action, let all the Devas lend their helping hands to you; what is inevitable must come to pass; what then can you care for your happiness or pain?
Ch 4 Verse 49-51 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सुखं क्षयाय पुण्यस्य दुःखं पापस्य मारिष । तस्मात्सुखक्षये हर्षः कर्तव्यः सर्वथा बुधैः ॥ ४९ ॥ अथवा मन्त्रयित्वाद्य कुरु यत्नं यथाविधि । कृते यत्ने महाराज भवितव्यं भविष्यति ॥ ५० ॥
Sanskrit Colophon
इति श्रीमद्देवीभागवते महापुराणेऽष्टादशसाहस्र्यां संहितायां पञ्चमस्कन्धे भयातुरेन्द्रादिदेवैः सुरगुरुणा सह परामर्शवर्णनं नाम चतुर्थोऽध्यायः ॥ ५.४॥
English Translation
O King! Happiness is felt for the expiation of good deeds and pain is felt for the expiation of bad deeds; therefore wise persons get thoroughly delighted when their puṇya ends. O King! Judge and hold a council to-day; then try your best. But what is unavoidable will come to pass, even if you try your best. Here ends the Fourth Chapter of the Fifth Book on the counsels given by Indra in the Māhā Purāṇam Śrī Mad Devī Bhāgavatam of 18,000 verses by Maharṣi Veda Vyāsa.
