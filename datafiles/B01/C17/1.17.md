# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 1, CHAPTER 17

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 1</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> १.१७ सप्तदशोऽध्यायः । शुकस्य राजमन्दिरप्रवेशवर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> सूत उवाच ।
> इत्युक्त्वा पितरं पुत्रः पादयोः पतितः शुकः ।
> बद्धाञ्जलिरुवाचेदं गन्तुकामो महामनाः ॥ १ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Sūta said :-- Thus speaking to his father about his intention to go to Miṭhilā, the high-souled Śūka Deva fell prostrate at his feet and with folded palms said :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 2-3</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> आपृच्छे त्वां महाभाग ग्राह्यं ते वचनं मया ।
> विदेहान्द्रष्टुमिच्छामि पालिताञ्जनकेन तु ॥ २ ॥
> 
> विना दण्डं कथं राज्यं करोति जनकः किल ।
> धर्मे न वर्तते लोको दण्डश्चेन्न भवेद्यदि ॥ ३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O highly fortunate one! Your word must be obeyed by me; now I desire to see, as you say, the kingdom of Janaka; kindly give me permission. O father! Again the doubt is coming within me how the king Janaka is governing his kingdom without sentencing any body? And if there be no punishment within his kingdom, no one will remain in the path of virtue.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 4</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> धर्मस्य कारणं दण्डो मन्वादिप्रहितः सदा ।
> स कथं वर्तते तात संशयोऽयं महान्मम ॥ ४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

It is for the sake of preserving religion that Manu and the other sages have always prescribed for punishment; how, then, religion can be preserved without inflicting punishment.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मम माता त्वियं वन्ध्या तद्वद्भाति विचेष्टितम् ।
> पृच्छामि त्वां महाभाग गच्छामि च परन्तप ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Mahābhāga! What you have spoken to me appears to me true like the sentence “My mother is barren.” So, O destroyer of foes! Permit and I will start for Miṭhilā.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 6</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सूत उवाच ।
> तं दृष्ट्वा गन्तुकामं च शुकं सत्यवतीसुतः ।
> आलिङ्ग्योवाच पुत्रं तं ज्ञानिनं निःस्पृहं दृढम् ॥ ६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Seeing the greatly wise son Śūka, void of any desires, earnestly anxious to go to Miṭhilā, gave him a cordial embrace and said :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> व्यास उवाच ।
> स्वस्त्यस्तु शक दीर्घायुर्भव पुत्र महामते ।
> सत्यां वाचं प्रदत्त्वा मे गच्छ तात यथासुखम् ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

“O highly intelligent son Śūka! Peace be on you! Have a long life. O child! Speak truly before me and go.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 8</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> आगन्तव्यं पुनर्गत्वा ममाश्रममनुत्तमम् ।
> न कुत्रापि च गन्तव्यं त्वया पुत्र कथञ्चन ॥ ८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O son! Say that after going to Miṭhilā you will come back again to this Āśrama; never that you will go anywhere else.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सुखं जीवामि पुत्राहं दृष्ट्वा ते मुखपङ्कजम् ।
> अपश्यन्दुःखमाप्नोमि प्राणस्त्वमसि मे सुत ॥ ९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O son! Seeing the lotus face of yours, I am passing my days happily; if I do not see you, I will suffer extreme pain. What more than this, that you are my life and soul.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 10</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> दृष्ट्वा त्वं जनकं पुत्र सन्देहं विनिवर्त्य च ।
> अत्रागत्य सुखं तिष्ठ वेदाध्ययनतत्परः ॥ १० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

I am saying, therefore, after seeing Janaka and clearing your doubts come here again and remain at peace, and study on the Vedas.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सूत उवाच ।
> इत्युक्तः सोऽभिवाद्यार्यं कृत्वा चैव प्रदक्षिणाम् ।
> चलितस्तरसातीव धनुर्मुक्तः शरो यथा ॥ ११ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa having spoken thus, Śūka bowed down and circumambulated his most worshipful father, and went out; he began to walk very fast like an arrow, leaving the bow, and when it has left the bow.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 12-14</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> सम्पश्यन्विविधान्देशाँल्लोकांश्च वित्तधर्मिणः ।
> वनानि पादपांश्चैव क्षेत्राणि फलितानि च ॥ १२ ॥
> 
> तापसांस्तप्यमानांश्च याजकान्दीक्षयान्वितान् ।
> योगाभ्यासरतान्योगिवानप्रस्थान्वनौकसः ॥ १३ ॥
> 
> शैवान्पाशुपतांश्चैव सौराञ्छाक्तांश्च वैष्णवान् ।
> वीक्ष्य नानाविधान्धर्माञ्जगामातिस्मयन्मुनिः ॥ १४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

On his journey he saw various countries, various classes of persons, earning money, various gardens and forests, various trees; in some places he saw fields with green grains and grains standing on them; at others he saw ascetics practising asceticism, and initiated Yājñiks (performing yajñas, or sacrifices); in some places he saw yogis practising yoga, the high-souled Vānaprasthīs (in the third stage of life) residing in the forest, and at others he saw devotees of Śiva, Śakti, Gaṇeśa, Sūryā and Viṣṇu and many others. Thus he went on in his journey, in great wonder, towards his destination.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 15</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वर्षद्वयेन मेरुं च समुल्लङ्घ्य महामतिः ।
> हिमाचलं च वर्षेण जगाम मिथिलां प्रति ॥ १५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

In his passage he crossed Meru in two years and the Mount Himālayās in one year and then reached the city Miṭhilā.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रविष्टो मिथिलां मध्ये पश्यन्सर्वर्द्धिमुत्तमाम् ।
> प्रजाश्च सुखिताः सर्वाः सदाचाराः सुसंस्थिताः ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Going there he found the place, full of wealth, corn, grains, etc., and all prosperities and the people were all very happy and they observed the rule of conduct as in the Śāstras.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 17-18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> क्षत्रा निवारितस्तत्र कस्त्वमत्र समागतः ।
> किं ते कार्यं वदस्वेति पृष्टस्तेन न चाब्रवीत् ॥ १७ ॥
> 
> निःसृत्य नगरद्वारात्स्थितः स्थाणुरिवाचलः ।
> विस्मितोऽतिहसंस्तस्थौ वचो नोवाच किञ्चन ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When he was about to enter into the city the guard in front of the gate stopped him, asking “Who are you? Sir. What for are you come here?” When the guard asked him thus, he replied nothing and went away to a distance and with great wonder could not help laughing in his mind and remained motionless like a statue.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 19</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रतीहार उवाच ।
> ब्रूहि मूकोऽसि किं ब्रह्मन्किमर्थं त्वमिहागतः ।
> चलनं च विना कार्यं न भवेदिति मे मतिः ॥ १९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

At this the guard said :-- “O Brāhman! Why have you remained silent? Kindly say what for you have come here? I know this well that no body goes anywhere without having something to do?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> राजाज्ञया प्रवेष्टव्यं नगरेऽस्मिन्सदा द्विज ।
> अज्ञातकुलशीलस्य प्रवेशो नात्र सर्वथा ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The king has forbidden strangers whose parentage and character are unknown. Therefore O Brāhmin! Every one has to take the king’s permission before he goes into the city.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 21</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तेजस्वी भासि नूनं त्वं ब्राह्मणो वेदवित्तमः ।
> कुलं कार्यं च मे ब्रूहि यथेष्टं गच्छ मानद ॥ २१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

It seems that you are a very energetic Brāhman and that you know the Vedas; so O giver of honour! speak out to me your object-in-view and go into the city as you like.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 22-23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शुक उवाच ।
> यदर्थमागतोऽस्म्यत्र तत्प्राप्तं वचनात्तव ।
> विदेहनगरं द्रष्टुं प्रवेशो यत्र दुर्लभः ॥ २२ ॥
> 
> मोहोऽयं मम दुर्बुद्धेः समुल्लङ्घ्य गिरिद्वयम् ।
> राजानं द्रष्टुकामोऽहं पर्यटन्समुपागतः ॥ २३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hearing these words of the guard, Śūka Deva began to say :-- “I have come to see the city of Videha Janaka; but now I see that persons like me find great difficulty to enter here; so O Gatekeeper! I have got the answer from you. I was a great fool that I was so much deluded that to see the king I crossed many countries and over topped the two mountains and have come here.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> वञ्चितोऽहं स्वयं पित्रा दूषणं कस्य दीयते ।
> भ्रामितोऽहं महाभाग कर्मणा वा महीतले ॥ २४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Mahābhāga! What blame can I put on others? It is my father that has deluded me; or my karma done in my previous birth is now making me wander about.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 25</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> धनाशा पुरुषस्येह परिभ्रमणकारणम् ।
> सा मे नास्ति तथाप्यत्र सम्प्राप्तोऽस्मि भ्रमात्किल ॥ २५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Alas! In this world greed for money is the sole cause to make a man knock about; but I have not got that even; my erroneous idea has brought me so far.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> निराशस्य सुखं नित्यं यदि मोहे न मज्जति ।
> निराशोऽहं महाभाग मग्नोऽस्मिन्मोहसागरे ॥ २६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

I now realise that a man, having no desires, gets constant happiness if he be not plunged in the net of delusion; else he cannot have any such. O Mahābhāga! Though I have no desire of anything, yet I am plunged in the sea of Moha.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> क्व मेरुर्मिथिला क्वेयं पद्भ्यां च समुपागतः ।
> परिश्रमफलं किं मे वञ्चितो विधिना किल ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Alas! Where is Meru? and where is Miṭhilā (a great distance intervenes) I have walked so great a distance on foot; alas! this is this the result of my so long a journey!

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 28</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रारब्धं किल भोक्तव्यं शुभं वाप्यथवाशुभम् ।
> उद्यमस्तद्वशे नित्यं कारयत्येव सर्वथा ॥ २८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Therefore I am thoroughly convinced that the Creator has deceived me. One must have to suffer for his Prārabdha karma, be it auspicious or inauspicious. One must make one’s effort, being always under the control of this Law of Karma. Though there be no apparent desire or cause, yet this Prārabdha Karma always puts a man into different actions.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न तीर्थं न च वेदोऽत्र यदर्थमिह मे श्रमः ।
> अप्रवेशः पुरे जातो विदेहो नाम भूपतिः ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

This place is not a Tīrath (holy place) nor there are the Vedas personified here, that I have taken so much pains and trouble to come here -- only there is one thing here and that is the king Janaka; but there is no chance to see him even; for I have not been able to enter even within his kingdom.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 30-32</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> इत्युक्त्या विररामाशु मौनीभूत इव स्थितः ।
> ज्ञातो हि प्रतिहारेण ज्ञानी कश्चिद्द्विजोत्तमः ॥ ३० ॥
> 
> सामपूर्वमुवाचासौ तं क्षत्ता संस्थितं मुनिम् ।
> गच्छ भो यत्र ते कार्यं यथेष्टं द्विजसत्तम ॥ ३१ ॥
> 
> अपराधो मम ब्रह्मन्यन्निवारितवानहम् ।
> तत्क्षन्तव्यं महाभाग विमुक्तानां क्षमा बलम् ॥ ३२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Thus saying, Śūka remained silent and began to stay as one who has taken the vow to remain silent. The guard then took him to be a very wise Brāhman and spoke in sweet words :-- “O Brāhman! Go to the place, as you like, where you have got your work. O Brāhman! I stopped you; so please excuse me for any offence iñcurred by me. Free persons like you have mercy alone as their greatest strength.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 33-34</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शुक उवाच ।
> किं तेऽत्र दूषणं क्षत्तः परतन्त्रोऽसि सर्वदा ।
> प्रभुकार्यं प्रकर्तव्यं सेवकेन यथोचितम् ॥ ३३ ॥
> 
> न भूपदूषणं चात्र यदहं रक्षितस्त्वया ।
> चोरशत्रुपरिज्ञानं कर्तव्यं सर्वथा बुधैः ॥ ३४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hearing this Śūka Deva said :--What is your fault? you are dependent on another; the servant ought to obey the words of his master and serve him by all means; and there is no fault of the king, too, in your not allowing me to enter; for the wise persons ought to ascertain by all means, whether the new comers are enemies or thieves?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 35</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ममैव सर्वथा दोषो यदहं समुपागतः ।
> गमनं परगेहे यल्लघुतायाश्च कारणम् ॥ ३५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hence when I am quite a stranger suddenly come to this place, that the fault is wholly mine. Every person knows that it is lowering oneself to go to another’s house.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 36</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रतीहार उवाच ।
> किं सुखं द्विज किं दुःखं किं कार्यं शुभमिच्छता ।
> कः शत्रुर्हितकर्ता को ब्रूहि सर्वं ममाद्य वै ॥ ३६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The guard then said :-- “O great Brāhman! what is happiness? and what is pain? what ought to be done to by your well wisher? who is your enemy? and who is your benefactor? Now advise me on all these points.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 37</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शुक उवाच ।
> द्वैविध्यं सर्वलोकेषु सर्वत्र द्विविधो जनः ।
> रागी चैव विरागी च तयोश्चित्तं द्विधा पुनः ॥ ३७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Hearing this Śūka Deva said :-- Everywhere men are divided, as far as their internal natures are concerned, into two classes; they are called attached or unattached. And the minds of these two classes are again of two kinds.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 38</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विरागी त्रिविधः कामं ज्ञातोऽज्ञातश्च मध्यमः ।
> रागी च द्विविधः प्रोक्तो मूर्खश्च चतुरस्तथा ॥ ३८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The “attached” man is stupid and cunning and the “unattached” is sub-divided into three classes knowing, unknowing and middling.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 39</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> चातुर्यं द्विविधं प्रोक्तं शास्त्रजं मतिजं तथा ।
> मतिस्तु द्विविधा लोके युक्तायुक्तेति सर्वथा ॥ ३९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The cunning man is divided again into two classes :-- Whether his cunningness is according to the dictates of Śāstras or arising from his intellect. Again intellect is sub-divided into two whether it is Yukta (one-pointed) or Ayukta (Diverted)

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 40</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> प्रतीहार उवाच ।
> यदुक्तं भवता विद्वन्नार्थज्ञोऽहं द्विजोत्तम ।
> तत्सर्वं विस्तरेणाद्य यथार्थं वद सत्तम ॥ ४० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The guard spoke :-- “O Learned one! I cannot understand what you say; so explain them to me what they mean.”

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 41</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शुक उवाच ।
> रागो यस्यास्ति संसारे स रागीत्युच्यते ध्रुवम् ।
> दुःखं बहुविधं तस्य सुखं च विविधं पुनः ॥ ४१ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Śūka Deva said :-- Those who are attached to this world are said to be “attached” persons. These attached persons feel frequently various pleasures and pains.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

When they get wives, sons, wealth, honour, rise, etc., they get pleasures; and if they do not get any of these they feel at every moment intense pain.

<details>

>
**English Translation:**

Now the attached person sought to take such means as will secure them the pleasures of this world; so whoever acts against those means are denominated as breakers of their happiness and so they are enemies; and whoever aids in their acquiring pleasures are denominated as their friends.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 44</b></summary>

>
**English Translation:**

Of these the attached but at the same time cunning man does not get confounded and bewildered by them; whereas stupid attached man gets always bewildered everywhere.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 45</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

The man that is dispassionate and engaged in determining the “self” dwells in a solitary place, meditates on “self”, finds pleasure in studying the Vedānta Śāstras and feels pain in all the topics on worldly affairs.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

The wise man that wants his real welfare and is averse to the worldly enjoyments finds that he has many enemies; lust, anger, palaces, etc., are his so many enemies. Contentment is his only friend in the three lokas and no one is his real self.

</details>

**English Translation:**

Hearing these words of Śūka Deva, the watchman considered Śūka Deva a very wise man and soon led him to a very beautiful compartment.

</details>

Śūka Deva then began to see that the town was full of three sorts of men, good, middling, and bad; and the shops were filled with various articles of merchandise.

<details>

**English Translation:**

The many things were being incessantly purchased and sold there. Within that town, filled with many men, money and all sorts wealth and prosperities, almost everywhere were seen instances of attachment, hatred, lust, anger, greed, vanity and delusion; at some parts there were seen persons quarrelling with each other.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 51-52</b></summary>

Seeing thus the three sorts of persons, the highly energetic Śūka, blazing like a second Sun went to the royal palace when the gateman stopped him. He stood there like a log of wood and began to meditate on “Moksa” (Liberation).

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 17 Verse 53-54</b></summary>

> **Sanskrit:**

He began to think the light and darkness as same; the greatly ascetic Śūka became merged in Dhyāna (meditation) and remained at one place motionless. In an instant, a royal minister came out and saluting him with folded hands, took him to a second compartment. Here the minister showed him beautiful divine gardens adorned nicely with rows of divine trees bearing fruits and gave him a good reception and took him to a very beautiful palace.

</details>

<details>

Those prostitutes then prepared various dishes, suited to the time and place, and sought the satisfaction of Śūka and then worshipped him duly with greatest devotion.

Śūka, finding pleasure in self and the controller of anger was not pleased or displeased with anything; so though be saw that the ladies were disturbed with amorous feelings, he remained quite undisturbed, calm and quiet.

<details>
