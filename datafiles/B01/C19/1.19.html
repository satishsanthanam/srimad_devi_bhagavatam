Śrīmad Devī Bhāgavatam

BOOK 1 • CHAPTER 19
On the description of the marriage of Śūka
← Previous Chapter ☰ Index Next Chapter →
Ch 19 Verse 1-2 Verse
Sanskrit Text
१.१९ एकोनविंशोऽध्यायः । शुकस्य विवाहादिकार्यवर्णनम् । शुक उवाच । सन्देहोऽयं महाराज वर्तते हृदये मम । मायामध्ये वर्तमानः स कथं निःस्पृहो भवेत् ॥ १ ॥ शास्त्रज्ञानं च सम्प्राप्य नित्यानित्यविचारणम् । त्यजते न मनो मोहं स कथं मुच्यते नरः ॥ २ ॥
English Translation
When even by the acquiring of wisdom of the Śāstras and the capability to judge which is real and which is unreal, the delusion of the mind is not dispelled until one resorts to the practice of Yoga, how then can freedom from desires and liberation come to a householder?
Ch 19 Verse 3 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अन्तर्गतं तमश्छेत्तुं शास्त्राद्बोधो हि न क्षमः । यथा न नश्यति तमः कृतया दीपवार्तया ॥ ३ ॥
English Translation
The darkness of a room is not destroyed by the mere mention of lamp, light; so the wisdom acquired by reading the Śāstras can never dispel the darkness of delusion that reigns in the inside of a man.
Ch 19 Verse 4 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अद्रोहः सर्वभूतेषु कर्तव्यः सर्वदा बुधैः । स कथं राजशार्दूल गृहस्थस्य भवेत्तथा ॥ ४ ॥
English Translation
O lion of kings! If one wants Mokṣa, one ought not to commit any act of revenge or injury or killing any being; how can this be possible to a householder?
Ch 19 Verse 5 Verse
Sanskrit Text
वित्तैषणा न ते शान्ता तथा राज्यसुखैषणा । जयैषणा च सङ्ग्रामे जीवन्मुक्तः कथं भवेः ॥ ५ ॥
English Translation
Your desires to acquire wealth, to enjoy royal pleasures and to get victory in battle have not yet subsided; how then can you be a Jivan mukta?
Ch 19 Verse 6 Verse
Sanskrit Text
चौरेषु चौरबुद्धिस्तु साधुबुद्धिस्तु तापसे । स्वपरत्वं तवाप्यस्ति विदेहस्त्वं कथं नृप ॥ ६ ॥
English Translation
O king! You consider yet a thief, thief and a saint, saint; you consider a man as your relative or other than that; these ideas have not vanished from you; how then can you be called Videha?
Ch 19 Verse 7-8 Verse
Sanskrit Text
कटुतीक्ष्णुकषायाम्लरसान्वेत्सि शुभाशुभान् । शुभेषु रमते चित्तं नाशुभेषु तथा नृप ॥ ७ ॥ जाग्रत्स्वप्नसुषुप्तिश्च तव राजन् भवन्ति हि । अवस्थास्तु यथाकालं तुरीया तु कथं नृप ॥ ८ ॥
English Translation
O king! You feel the pungent, bitter, astringent, sour tastes and the like; you feel good and bad rasas respectively; you become glad when success comes to you and you feel sorrow when you happen to fail; and you experience the three states, waking, dreaming, and deep sleep as an ordinary man does, how then can you be called to attain the Turīya (fourth) state? May I ask :--
Ch 19 Verse 9 Verse
Sanskrit Text
पदात्यश्वरथेभाश्च सर्वे वै वशगा मम । स्वाम्यहं चैव सर्वेषां मन्यसे त्वं न मन्यसे ॥ ९ ॥
English Translation
Whether you cherish this idea that all these infantries, cavalries, chariots, and that all these elephants are mine; I am the lord of all the wealth and things? Or whether you do not cherish this idea?
Ch 19 Verse 10 Verse
Sanskrit Text
मिष्टमत्सि सदा राजन्मुदितो विमनास्तथा । मालायां च तथा सर्पे समदृक् क्व नृपोत्तम ॥ १० ॥
English Translation
O king! I think you eat sweet and good things, and, at times, feel pleasure and, at other times, feel pain! So, O king! How can you look on the garland of flowers and the snake as one and the same?
Ch 19 Verse 11 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विमुक्तस्तु भवेद्राजन् समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः । एकात्मबुद्धिः सर्वत्र हितकृत्सर्वजन्तुषु ॥ ११ ॥
English Translation
O king! He who is a Muktapuruṣa considers a lump of earth, a piece of stone, and gold as of one and the same value; he considers everything to be the same Ātmān and does good to all the beings.
Ch 19 Verse 12 Verse
Sanskrit Text
न मेऽद्य रमते चित्तं गृहदारादिषु क्वचित् । एकाकी निःस्पृहोऽत्यर्थं चरेयमिति मे मतिः ॥ १२ ॥
English Translation
Whatever that may be, I do not find any pleasure at present with houses, wife, etc., or with anything, in fact. What my heart’s desire is that I roam alone always without any desires in my heart.
Ch 19 Verse 13 Verse
Sanskrit Text
निःसङ्गो निर्ममः शान्तः पत्रमूलफलाशनः । मृगवद्विचरिष्यामि निर्द्वन्द्वो निष्परिग्रहः ॥ १३ ॥
English Translation
Therefore I like not to have any companion; to be free from any attachment and to be peaceful, and calm; I do not wish to accept anything from anybody; I will forego all pleasures and pains from cold, warmth, etc., and I will sustain my life on roots, fruits, and leaves, obtained without any effort and will roam, as I like, like a deer.
Ch 19 Verse 14 Verse
Sanskrit Text
किं मे गृहेण वित्तेन भार्यया च सुरूपया । विरागमनसः कामं गुणातीतस्य पार्थिव ॥ १४ ॥
English Translation
When I have not got the least attachment to the household life and when I am beyond all the attributes, what necessity have I then of house, wealth or a suitable wife?
Ch 19 Verse 15 Verse
Sanskrit Text
चिन्त्यसे विविधाकारं नानारागसमाकुलम् । दम्भोऽयं किल ते भाति विमुक्तोऽस्मीति भाषसे ॥ १५ ॥
English Translation
And when you think of various things with loving heart, and yet say that you are a Jivan mukta, that is nothing but a mere vanity of yours!
Ch 19 Verse 16 Verse
Sanskrit Text
कदाचिच्छत्रुजा चिन्ता धनजा च कदाचन । कदाचित्सैन्यजा चिन्ता निश्चिन्तोऽसि कदा नृप ॥ १६ ॥
English Translation
O king! When you think and become anxious about your enemies, about your wealth or sometimes about your army, how then can you be said to be free from cares?
Ch 19 Verse 17 Verse
Sanskrit Text
वैखानसा ये मुनयो मिताहारा जितव्रताः । तेऽपि मुह्यन्ति संसारे जानन्तोऽपि ह्यसत्यताम् ॥ १७ ॥
English Translation
What more can be said than the fact that many Munis, eating moderately and controlling their senses, and leading an anchorite’s life, and knowing the unreality of the world, fall victims to the Māyā!
Ch 19 Verse 18-19 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तव वंशसमुत्थानां विदेहा इति भूपते । कुटिलं नाम जानीहि नान्यथेति कदाचन ॥ १८ ॥ विद्याधरो यथा मूर्खो जन्मान्धस्तु दिवाकरः । लक्ष्मीधरो दरिद्रश्च नाम तेषां निरर्थकम् ॥ १९ ॥
English Translation
Then what need there is to talk of you? O king! know that the hereditary title “Videha” to your line of kings indicates downright insincerity; nothing can be other than this as the name “Vidyā Dhara” (holder of knowledge) is applied to an illiterate man; as the name “Divākara” (sun) is given to a born-blind man, as the name “Lakṣmīdhara” (holder of wealth) is given to even a poor man, as these names are quite useless to me.
Ch 19 Verse 20-21 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तव वंशोद्भवा ये ये श्रुताः पूर्वे मया नृपाः । विदेहा इति विख्याता नामतः कर्मतो न ते ॥ २० ॥ निमिनामाभवद्राजा पूर्वं तव कुले नृप । यज्ञार्थं स तु राजर्षिर्वसिष्ठं स्वगुरुं मुनिम् ॥ २१ ॥
English Translation
I have heard that the kings of your family who were your predecessors were called “Videha” in name only and not in deed. O king! In your family there reigned a king named “Nimi.” Once on a time that royal sage invited his Guru Vaśiṣṭha to perform a sacrifice, when Vaṣ’iṣṭha said :--
Ch 19 Verse 22-23 Verse
Sanskrit Text
निमन्त्रयामास तदा तमुवाच नृपं मुनिः । निमन्त्रितोऽस्मि यज्ञार्थं देवेन्द्रेणाधुना किल ॥ २२ ॥ कृत्वा तस्य मखं पूर्णं करिष्यामि तवापि वै । तावत्कुरुष्व राजेन्द्र सम्भारं तु शनैः शनैः ॥ २३ ॥
English Translation
:-- “I am already invited by Indra, the lord of the Devas, to perform his sacrifice; so O king! let me first finish his work; I will then take up your work. Better go on collecting the sacrificial materials till my that work is complete.”
Ch 19 Verse 24 Verse
Sanskrit Text
इत्युक्त्या निर्ययौ सोऽथ महेन्द्रयजने मुनिः । निमिरन्यं गुरुं कृत्वा चकार मखमुत्तमम् ॥ २४ ॥
English Translation
Thus saying, Vaṣ’iṣṭha went away to perform Indra’s sacrifice; on the other hand, the royal sage Nimi selected another priest and made him his Guru and began his sacrifice.
Ch 19 Verse 25 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तच्छ्रुत्वा कुपितोऽत्यर्थं वसिष्ठो नृपतिं पुनः । शशाप च पतत्वद्य देहस्ते गुरुलोपक ॥ २५ ॥
English Translation
Hearing all this, Maharṣi Vaṣ’iṣṭha became angry and cursed him thus :-- “O forsaker of your Guru! For the crime of forsaking your Guru, let thy body be destroyed today!”
Ch 19 Verse 26 Verse
Sanskrit Text
राजापि तं शशापाथ तवापि च पतत्वयम् । अन्योन्यशापात्पतितौ तावेव च मया श्रुतम् ॥ २६ ॥
English Translation
At this, the royal sage, too, cursed Vaṣ’iṣṭha in his turn “Let your body fall off also.” Then the bodies of both the persons fell.
Ch 19 Verse 27 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विदेहेन च राजेन्द्र कथं शप्तो गुरुः स्वयम् । विनोद इव मे चित्ते विभाति नृपसत्तम ॥ २७ ॥
English Translation
But, O king! this curiousity came to my mind, how the royal sage, whose body fell before, cursed his own Guru afterwards.
Ch 19 Verse 28 Verse
Sanskrit Text
जनक उवाच । सत्यमुक्तं त्वया नात्र मिथ्या किञ्चिदिदं मतम् । तथापि श‍ृणु विप्रेन्द्र गुरुर्मम सुपूजितः ॥ २८ ॥
English Translation
Janaka said :-- O Lord of Brāhmins! what you have said is, in my opinion, all quite true; nothing is false. Still hear. Know what my most worshipful Guru Deva has spoken to me is, in fact, true (and nothing else).
Ch 19 Verse 29-30 Verse
Sanskrit Text
पितुः सङ्गं परित्यज्य त्वं वनं गन्तुमिच्छसि । मृगैः सह सुसम्बन्धो भविता ते न संशयः ॥ २९ ॥ महाभूतानि सर्वत्र निःसङ्गः क्व भविष्यसि । आहारार्थं सदा चिन्ता निश्चिन्तः स्याः कथं मुने ॥ ३० ॥
English Translation
You are now intending to quit the company of your father and go to the forest; well and good! but even then you will undoubtedly have the company of deer, etc.; see, also, that when the five elements, earth, water, air, etc., are present, encompassing everywhere, how, then, can you expect to be free from all companions? So, O Muni! when you will have to think always of your food, how, then, can you be said to be free from all cares?
Ch 19 Verse 31-32 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दण्डाजिनकृता चिन्ता यथा तव वनेऽपि च । तथैव राज्यचिन्ता मे चिन्तयानस्य वा न वा ॥ ३१ ॥ विकल्पोपहतस्त्वं वै दूरदेशमुपागतः । न मे विकल्पसन्देहो निर्विकल्पोऽस्मि सर्वथा ॥ ३२ ॥
English Translation
Again, even if you go to the forest, you will have to think there also for your staff, deer skin, etc.; so you can take my case, too, of thinking of my kingdom, whether I think or not, as your thinking of staff, deer skin, etc., your heart is tainted with Vikalpa Jñān (knowledge of doubt, duality, etc.); and therefore you have come here from a far-off country. But my heart is free from any such doubt and I am remaining quite cheerful here.
Ch 19 Verse 33 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सुखं स्वपिमि विप्राहं सुखं भुञ्जामि सर्वथा । न बद्धोऽस्मीति बुद्ध्याहं सर्वदैव सुखी मुने ॥ ३३ ॥
English Translation
O best of Brāhmins! I have got no doubt whatsoever on any point, and therefore I take my food and go to sleep with great pleasure. “I am not bound up by this world” this idea gives me constant happiness of the highest degree.
Ch 19 Verse 34 Verse
Sanskrit Text
त्वं तु दुःखी सदैवासि बद्धोऽहमिति शङ्कया । इति शङ्कां परित्यज्य सुखी भव समाहितः ॥ ३४ ॥
English Translation
But you consider that you are bound and therefore you feel always constant pain. So leave off your idea that you are bound, and be happy.
Ch 19 Verse 35 Verse
Sanskrit Text
देहोऽयं मम बन्धोऽयं न ममेति च मुक्तता । तथा धनं गृहं राज्यं न ममेति च निश्चयः ॥ ३५ ॥
English Translation
“This body is mine” this knowledge leads to my bondage; and “This body is not mine” this knowledge leads to freedom so know this verily that all this wealth, kingdom, etc., are not mine.
Ch 19 Verse 36-37 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सूत उवाच । तच्छ्रुत्वा वचनं तस्य शुकः प्रीतमनाभवत् । आपृच्छ्य तं जगामाशु व्यासस्याश्रममुत्तमम् ॥ ३६ ॥ आगच्छन्तं सुतं दृष्ट्वा व्यासोऽपि सुखमाप्तवान् । आलिङ्ग्याघ्राय मूर्धानं पप्रच्छ कुशलं पुनः ॥ ३७ ॥
English Translation
Sūta said :-- Hearing these words of the royal sage, Śūka Deva became exceedingly glad and pronounced “Sadhu” “Sādhu” (true saint, indeed a true saint, well said) and went away without any delay to the pleasant Āśrama of Vyāsa. Vyāsa, too, seeing his son come back, became very glad and embraced him and took the smell of his head and asked about his welfare again
Ch 19 Verse 38-40 Verse
Sanskrit Text
स्थितस्तत्राश्रमे रम्ये पितुः पार्श्वे समाहितः । वेदाध्ययनसम्पन्नः सर्वशास्त्रविशारदः ॥ ३८ ॥ जनकस्य दशां दृष्ट्वा राज्यस्थस्य महात्मनः । स निर्वृतिं परां प्राप्य पितुराश्रमसंस्थितः ॥ ३९ ॥ पितॄणां सुभगा कन्या पीवरी नाम सुन्दरी । शुकश्चकार पत्नीं तां योगमार्गस्थितोऽपि हि ॥ ४० ॥
English Translation
Then Śūka Deva, well conversant with the Śāstras and ever ready in studying the Vedas, sat by the side of his father, with an enlightened mind, in his lovely Āśrama and thinking of the state of the highsouled Janaka in his kingdom, began to feel the highest peace. Though Śūka adopted the path of Yoga, yet he married the daughter of a Muni, named Pivarī, very beautiful, fortunate, enhancing the glory of her father’s family.
Ch 19 Verse 41-45 Verse
Sanskrit Text
स तस्यां जनयामास पुत्रांश्चतुर एव हि । कृष्णं गौरप्रभं चैव भूरिं देवश्रुतं तथा ॥ ४१ ॥ कन्यां कीर्तिं समुत्पाद्य व्यासपुत्रः प्रतापवान् । ददौ विभ्राजपुत्राय त्वणहाय महात्मने ॥ ४२ ॥ अणुहस्य सुतः श्रीमान्ब्रह्मदत्तः प्रतापवान् । ब्रह्मज्ञः पृथिवीपालः शककन्यासमुद्भवः ॥ ४३ ॥ कालेन कियता तत्र नारदस्योपदेशतः । ज्ञानं परमकं प्राप्य योगमार्गमनुत्तमम् ॥ ४४ ॥ पुत्रे राज्यं निधायाथ गतो बदरिकाश्रमम् । मायाबीजोपदेशेन तस्य ज्ञानं निरर्गलम् ॥ ४५ ॥
English Translation
Then were born first the four sons named Krishna, Gauraprabha, Bhūri, and Devaśruta out of the sperm of Śūka and the ovum of Pivari; and next a daughter was born named Kīrti of them. Vyāsa’s son Śūka, endowed with the fire of asceticism gave the daughter Kīrti in marriage in due time with the high-souled Anūha, the son of Vibhrāja. As time passed on, a son was born of the womb of Kīrti and the sperm of Anūha, a son who became the powerful king Brahmadatta, the knower of Brahmā and endowed with wealth and prosperity. Some time elapsed when Anūha, the son-in-law of Śūka Deva, getting from Nārada the Māyāvīja and highest knowledge of Yoga handed over his kingdom to his son and went to the hermitage of Vadarikā and became liberated. The Devarṣī Nārada gave him the mantra, the vīja of Māyā; and by the influence of that mantra and by the grace of the Devī, the knowledge of the Supreme Brahmā, arose in him without any obstacle and gave him liberation.
Ch 19 Verse 46 Verse
Sanskrit Text
नारदस्य प्रसादेन जातं सद्यो विमुक्तिदम् । कैलासशिखरे रम्ये त्यक्त्वा सङ्गं पितुः शुकः ॥ ४६ ॥
English Translation
On the other hand Śūka Deva, always averse to any company, left his father and went to the beautiful mountain Kailāśa. He began to meditate on the unmoving Brahmā and thus remained there.
Ch 19 Verse 47 Verse
Sanskrit Text
ध्यानमास्थाय विपुलं स्थितः सङ्गपराङ्मुखः । उत्पपात गिरेः श‍ृङ्गात्सिद्धिं च परमां गतः ॥ ४७ ॥
English Translation
After some time the highly energetic Śūka Deva attained Siddhi (supernatural powers) Animā, Laghimā, etc., rose up high in the air from the top of the mountain and began to roam there, and then he appeared like a second Sun.
Ch 19 Verse 48-49 Verse
Sanskrit Text
आकाशगो महातेजा विरराज यथा रविः । गिरेः श‍ृङ्गं द्विधा जातं शुकस्योत्पतने तदा ॥ ४८ ॥ उत्पाता बहवो जाताः शुकश्चाकाशगोऽभवत् । अन्तरिक्षे यथा वायुः स्तूयमानः सुरर्षिभिः ॥ ४९ ॥
English Translation
When Śūka arose from the peak, it severed into two and various ominous signs became visible. When Śūka Deva, appearing like a second Sun by the dazzling brilliancy of his body, suddenly vanished away like air and became diluted in the Paramātman, entering into everything and became invisible, then the Devarṣis began to chant hymns to him.
Ch 19 Verse 50-51 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तेजसातिविराजन्वै द्वितीय इव भास्करः । व्यासस्तु विरहाक्रान्तः क्रन्दन्पुत्रेति चासकृत् ॥ ५० ॥ गिरेः श‍ृङ्गे गतस्तत्र शुको यत्र स्थितोऽभवत् । क्रन्दमानं तदा दीनं व्यासं मत्वा श्रमाकुलम् ॥ ५१ ॥
English Translation
On the other hand, Vyāsa Deva became very much distressed with the separation from his son and cried out frequently “Oh, my son! Alas! my son Where are you gone?” and went to the summit of the mountain where Śūka did go and wept bitterly. Then Śūka Deva, who was then residing as the Paramātman, the Internal controller of all the beings and with all the beings, knowing Vyāsa Deva as very much fatigued, distressed, and crying, spoke out as an echo from the mountains and trees thus :-- “O Father! There is no difference between you and me, considered in the light of Ātman; then why are you weeping for me?”
Ch 19 Verse 52 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सर्वभूतगतः साक्षी प्रतिशब्दमदात्तदा । तत्राद्यापि गिरेः श‍ृङ्गे प्रतिशब्दः स्फुटोऽभवत् ॥ ५२ ॥
English Translation
Even today the above echo is clearly heard (almost daily). Seeing Vyāsa Deva grieved very much for the separation from his son and always crying
Ch 19 Verse 53-54 Verse
Sanskrit Text
रुदन्तं तं समालक्ष्य व्यासं शोकसमन्वितम् । पुत्र पुत्रेति भाषन्तं विरहेण परिप्लुतम् ॥ ५३ ॥ शिवस्तत्र समागत्य पाराशर्यमबोधयत् । व्यास शोकं मा कुरु त्वं पुत्रस्ते योगवित्तमः ॥ ५४ ॥
English Translation
Oh! my son!” Bhagavān Maheśvara came there and consoled him saying “O Vyāsa Deva! your son is the foremost of the Yogis; he has attained the highest state, so very rare to the ordinary persons that are not self controlled. So do not be sorry any more.
Ch 19 Verse 55 Verse
Sanskrit Text
परमां गतिमापन्नो दुर्लभां चाकृतात्मभिः । तस्य शोको न कर्तव्यस्त्वयाशोकं विजानता ॥ ५५ ॥
English Translation
O Sinless One! when you have realised the Brahmā-tattva, then you ought not to express any sorrow for your Śūka who is now stationed in that Brāhman. Your fame is now unrivalled, only on account of your having got a son like him.”
Ch 19 Verse 56 Verse
Sanskrit Text
कीर्तिस्ते विपुला जाता तेन पुत्रेण चानघ । व्यास उवाच । न शोको याति देवेश किं करोमि जगत्पते ॥ ५६ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa Deva said :-- “O Lord of the Devās! O Lord of the world! What am I to do now? My grief does not quit my heart anyhow or other. My eyes are as yet satisfied in seeing my son; they like still to see the son.”
Ch 19 Verse 57 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अतृप्ते लोचने मेऽद्य पुत्रदर्शनलालसे । महादेव उवाच । छायां द्रक्ष्यसि पुत्रस्य पार्श्वस्थां सुमनोहराम् ॥ ५७ ॥
English Translation
Hearing these sorrowful words of Vyāsa, Bhagāvan Mahādeva said :-- “O Muni Sārdula! I grant this boon to you that you will see the form of your son abiding in shadow, very beautiful, by the side of you.
Ch 19 Verse 58-61 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तां वीक्ष्य मुनिशार्दूल शोकं जहि परन्तप । सूत उवाच । तदा ददर्श व्यासस्तु छायां पुत्रस्य सुप्रभाम् ॥ ५८ ॥ दत्त्वा वरं हरस्तस्मै तत्रैवान्तरधीयत । अन्तर्हिते महादेवे व्यासः स्वाश्रममभ्यगात् ॥ ५९ ॥ शुकस्य विरहेणापि तप्तः परमदुःखितः ॥ ६० ॥
Sanskrit Colophon
इति श्रीमद्देवीभागवते महापुराणेऽष्टादशसाहस्र्यां संहितायां प्रथमस्कन्धे शुकस्य विवाहादिकार्यवर्णनं नामैकोनविंशोऽध्यायः ॥ १.१९॥
English Translation
O Destroyer of enemies! Now abandon your grief by seeing that shadow form of your son.” When Bhagavān Maheśvara said so, Vyāsa began to see the bright shade form of his son. Granting thus the boon, Bhagavān Mahādeva vanished then and there. When He vanished away, Vyāsa became very much distressed with sorrow for the bereavement of his son and returned with heavy heart to his own hermitage. Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the first Skandha on the description of the marriage of Śūka in the Mahāpurāṇa Śrī Mad Devī Bhāgavatam of 18,000 verses.
