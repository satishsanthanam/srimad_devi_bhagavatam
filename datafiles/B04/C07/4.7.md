# SRIMAD DEVI BHAGAVATAM — BOOK 4, CHAPTER 7

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 1</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> ४.७ सप्तमोऽध्यायः । अहङ्कारावर्तनवर्णनम् ।
> 
> 
> व्यास उवाच ।
> इत्याकर्ण्य वचस्तासां धर्मपुत्रः प्रतापवान् ।
> विमर्शमकरोच्चित्ते किं कर्तव्यं मयाधुना ॥ १ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O king! The Dharma’s son, of excellent prowess, hearing thus, the words of these damsels, thought within himself, thus :-- what shall I do under the above circumstances.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 2</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> हास्योऽहं मुनिवृन्देषु भविष्याम्यद्य सङ्गमात् ।
> अहङ्कारादिदं प्राप्तं दुःखं नात्र विचारणा ।
> मूलं धर्मविनाशस्य प्रथमं यदहङ्कृतिः ॥ २ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

If I indulge now in sexual pleasures, I will be an object of laughter amongst the Munis. This present trouble has, no doubt, arisen from my Ahamkāra (egoism). This Ahamkāra is the first and foremost in ruining one’s Dharma.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 3-5</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मूलं संसारवृक्षस्य यतः प्रोक्तो महात्मभिः ।
> दृष्ट्वा मौनं समाधाय न स्थितोऽहं समागतम् ॥ ३ ॥
> 
> वाराङ्गनागणं जुष्टं तेनासं दुःखभाजनम् ।
> उत्पादितास्तथा नार्यो मया धर्मव्ययेन वै ॥ ४ ॥
> 
> तास्तु मां बाधितुं वृत्ताः कामार्ताः प्रमदोत्तमाः ।
> ऊर्णनाभिरिवाद्याहं जालेन स्वकृतेन वै ॥ ५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

The wise sages have declared this Ahamkāra as the root of this tree of world. I did not observe the vow of silence on seeing those damsels come here; I have held much conversations with them; therefore I have got into this troublesome anxiety and sorrow. I have created these damsels at the expense of my Dharma and Tapasyā. The beautiful and lovely damsels sent by Indra are now full of lust; and are bent on ruining my tapasayā. Now if through Ahamkāra I had not created the damsels, I would not have fallen into this difficulty. Now I am caught firmly in the meshes of my own creation like a spider; what am I to do next!

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 6-7</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> बद्धोऽस्मि सुदृढेनात्र किं कर्तव्यमतः परम् ।
> यदि चिन्तां समुत्सृज्य सन्त्यजाम्यबला इमाः ॥ ६ ॥
> 
> शप्त्वा भ्रष्टा व्रजिष्यन्ति सर्वा भग्नमनोरथाः ।
> मुक्तोऽहं सञ्चरिष्यामि विजने परमं तपः ॥ ७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

If I abandon these damsels, thinking that there is no necessity to reconsider the matter, then these would be broken hearted; and they would go away cursing me? Yet I would be free from this present danger at least and then be able to practise excellent tapasyā in a lonely place. Therefore, now, I will get angry and tell these damsels go away from me.

</details>

>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O King! The Muni Nārāyaṇa thought that he would become thus happy; but, at the next moment, he discussed in his mind thus :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 9</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विमर्शमकरोच्चित्ते सुखोत्पादनसाधने ।

>
**English Translation:**

The second great enemy is anger; it is greater than causing hurt to others; and it is is greater than lust aud avarice.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 10</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

Out of anger people commit murder; this murder is the source of hell and is giving pains to all.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 11</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

As trees, rubbing against each other, generate fire; and are themselves burnt up in this fire, so fire arising from this body ultimately burns this body to death.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- The younger brother Nara on seeing his elder brother anxious and low-spirited, spoke out what is right, as follows :--

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 13</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> उवाच वचनं तथ्यं नरो धर्मसुतोऽनुजः ।
> नर उवाच ।
> नारायण महाभाग कोपं यच्छ महामते ॥ १३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Nārāyaṇa! You are very intelligent and very good; therefore reliquish this feeling of anger and betake to quietude and peace, and kill the dreadful anger.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 14-15</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> शान्तं भावं समाश्रित्य नाशयाहङ्कृतिं पराम् ।
> पुराहङ्कारदोषेण तपो नष्टं किलावयोः ॥ १४ ॥
> 
> सङ्ग्रामश्चाभवत्ताभ्यां भावाभ्यामसुरेण ह ।
> दिव्यवर्षसहस्रं तु प्रह्लादेन महाद्भुतम् ॥ १५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Do you not remember that it is through this Ahamkāra and anger that our tapasyā was destroyed on a previous occasion; and we had to fight severely with Prahlāda, the Lord of the Asuras for one full divine thousand years.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 16</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> दुःखं बहुतरं प्राप्तं तत्रावाभ्यां सुरोत्तम ।
> तस्मात्क्रोधं परित्यज्य शान्तो भव मुनीश्वर ॥ १६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

O Lord of the Devas! We were put to much difficulties then; therefore O Lord of the Munis! Get rid of this anger; be quiet! The sages declare the peace is the root cause and the only object of Tapasyā.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 17</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> (शान्तत्वं तपसो मूलं मुनिभिः परिकीर्तितं ।)॥
> 
> व्यास उवाच ।
> इति तस्य वचः श्रुत्वा शान्तोऽभूद्धर्मनन्दनः ।
> जनमेजय उवाच ।
> संशयोऽयं मुनिश्रेष्ठ प्रह्लादेन महात्मना ॥ १७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- On hearing these words of his younger brother Nara, the Dharma’s son Nārāyaṇa took to peace.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 18</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> विष्णुभक्तेन शान्तेन कथं युद्धं कृतं पुरा ।
> कृतवन्तौ कथं युद्धं नरनारायणावृषी ॥ १८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Janamejaya said :-- O Lord of the Munis! The high souled Prahlāda was a devotee of Viṣṇu and of a peace loving heart: how it was that, in the ancient days, the battle took place between him and these Ṛṣis; how could the Ṛṣis fight? There is this great doubt in my mind.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 19-20</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तापसौ धर्मपुत्रौ द्वौ सुशान्तमानसावुभौ ।
> समागमः कथं जातस्तयोर्दैत्यसुतस्य च ॥ १९ ॥
> 
> सङ्ग्रामस्तु कथं ताभ्यां कृतस्तेन महात्मना ।
> प्रह्लादोऽप्यतिधर्मात्मा ज्ञानवान्विष्णुतत्परः ॥ २० ॥
>
**English Translation:**

These two Dharma’s sons were ascetics and peace loving; how the fight could come to pass between these and the Daityāsuras? How did these two Ṛṣis fight with the high souled Prahlāda?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 21-22</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> नरनारायणौ तद्वत्तापसौ सत्त्वसंस्थितौ ।
> तेन ताभ्यां समुद्भूतं वैरं यदि परस्परम् ॥ २१ ॥
> 
> तदा तपसि धर्मे च श्रम एव हि केवलम् ।
> क्व जपः क्व तपश्चर्या पुरा सत्ययुगेऽपि च ॥ २२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Prahlāda was very religious, full of knowledge and very much devoted to Viṣṇu. Nara Nārāyaṇa were Sattvik and ascetics; therefore if there had occurred enmity between those, it appears that the religion and asceticism, Tapasyā and Dharma were matters in name only; and the labour was spent in vain in the golden age even. What was the value of asceticism and meditation and muttering silently the mantras! No one can make out.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 23</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तादृशैर्न जितं चित्तं क्रोधाहङ्कारसंवृतम् ।
> न क्रोधो न च मात्सर्यमहङ्काराङ्कुरं विना ॥ २३ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Oh! Persons like them could not conquer their hearts full of anger and egoism! Anger and jealousy cannot spring unless there be at the bottom a feeling of egoism (Ahamkāra).

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 24</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अहङ्कारात्समुत्पन्नाः कामक्रोधादयः किल ।
> वर्षकोटिसहस्रं तु तपः कृत्वातिदारुणम् ॥ २४ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

All the passions, lust, greed, anger, etc., come out of Ahamkāra (egoism), there is no doubt of it; one hundred lakh years of severe asceticism are rendered quite useless by the cropping up afterwards of a bit of Ahamkāra.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 25</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> अहङ्काराङ्कुरे जाते व्यर्थं भवति सर्वथा ।
> यथा सूर्योदये जाते तमोरूपं न तिष्ठति ॥ २५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

As darkness is dispelled entirely on sunrise, so no trace of religious merit can exist on the rising of a bit of Ahamkāra.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 26</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

When Prahlāda could fight with Śrī Bhagavān Hari, then, Oh! all his merits in this world are rendered of no use whatsoever.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 27</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> तदा व्यर्थं कृतं सर्वं सुकृतं किल भूतले ।
> नरनारायणौ शान्तौ विहाय परमं तपः ॥ २७ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Where is the religious merit and where is peace when the quiet souled persons Nara Nārāyaṇa, the two Ṛṣis began to fight, without paying any heed to their highest end, the Tapasyā?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 28</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> कृतवन्तौ यदा युद्धं क्व शमः सुकृतं पुनः ।
> ईदृग्भ्यां सत्त्वयुक्ताभ्यामजेया यद्यहङ्कृतिः ॥ २८ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

When Ahamkāra became invincible by the two Ṛṣis, then what can be expected from the weak trivial persons like us in the matter of subjugating this Ahamkāra?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 29</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मादृशानाञ्ज का वार्ता मुनेऽहङ्कारसङ्क्षये ।
> अहङ्कारपरित्यक्तो न कोऽप्यस्ति जगत्त्रये ॥ २९ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Who can be free from Ahamkāra in these three worlds, when the high souled persons like the above were not free from it? I am now quite confident that, in this Universe, no body was ever before free from Ahamkāra nor will there be any such in the distant future.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 30</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**

>
**English Translation:**

One can be free if bound by an iron or a wooden chain; but when one is pierced by Ahamkāra, one can never become free from it.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 31</b></summary>

**English Translation:**

This whole Universe, moving and unmoving, is rolling in this Samsāra (migration and transmigration) polluted by urine and faeces, being covered by Ahamkāra.

</details>

<details>

Where is, then the Brahmā Jñana? O Good One in vows! The Karma theory, according to the Mimāmsakas, seems reasonable and true.

</details>

<details>

>
**English Translation:**

O Muni! What can you expect from the weak-minded persons like me in this Kali yuga, when the great persons are always overpowered with lust, anger, etc.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 34-35</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> मादृशानां कलावस्मिन्का कथा मुनिसत्तम ।
> व्यास उवाच ।
> कार्यं वै कारणाद्भिन्नं कथं भवति भारत ॥ ३४ ॥
> 
> कटकं कुण्डलञ्चैव सुवर्णसदृशं भवेत् ।
> अहङ्कारोद्भवं सर्वं ब्रह्माण्डं सचराचरम् ॥ ३५ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Vyāsa said :-- O Descendant of Bharata! How can the effect be different from its cause? Gold and golden ear-rings though different in form owing to upādhis, are both similar to their original cause, the metal gold. Thread is the cause of cloth; therefore as cloth cannot be different from its thread, so this whole universe, moving and unmoving, is sprung from Ahamkāra; then how can it be free from Ahamkāra?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 36</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> पटस्तन्तुवशः प्रोक्तस्तद्वियुक्तः कथं भवेत् ।
> मायागुणैस्त्रिभिः सर्वं रचितं स्थिरजङ्गमम् ॥ ३६ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

All this, moving and unmoving, including a blade of grass, are fashioned out of the three qualities of Māyā; so if it be formed of those qualities, what repentance can come to those who are wise and know every phenomenon as unreal?

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 37</b></summary>

>
**English Translation:**

O Best of kings! Brahmā, Viṣṇu or Maheśa, even these are all rolling in this vast ocean of Samsāra, being bewildered and fascinated by Ahamkāra.

</details>

The great sages like Vaśiṣṭha, Nārada and the other Munis are frequently taking their births in this Samsāra.

</details>

<details>

**English Translation:**

In this Trilokī, there is not even one embodied soul, who is entirely free from this Māyā and has become quiet and immersed in the high bliss of the Supreme Self.

<details>

**English Translation:**

O Best of kings! Lust, anger, avarice, and fascination, all, arise from Ahamkāra. These do not leave any embodied person.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>Ch 7 Verse 41-42</b></summary>
  <br>

> **Sanskrit:**
> न मुञ्चन्ति नरं सर्वं देहवन्तं नृपोत्तम ।
> अधीत्य वेदशास्त्राणि पुराणानि विचिन्त्य च ॥ ४१ ॥
> 
> कृत्वा तीर्थाटनं दानं ध्यानञ्चैव सुरार्चनम् ।
> करोति विषयासक्तः सर्वं कर्म च चौरवत् ॥ ४२ ॥
>
**English Translation:**

Studying all the Vedas and Purāṇas, going to all the sacred places pilgrimages, making charities, thinking on Paramātman and worshipping the gods, doing all these, the people still get attached to sensual objects and act like a thief.
