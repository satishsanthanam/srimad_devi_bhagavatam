Ch 7 Verse 1 Verse
Sanskrit Text
४.७ सप्तमोऽध्यायः । अहङ्कारावर्तनवर्णनम् । व्यास उवाच । इत्याकर्ण्य वचस्तासां धर्मपुत्रः प्रतापवान् । विमर्शमकरोच्चित्ते किं कर्तव्यं मयाधुना ॥ १ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- O king! The Dharma’s son, of excellent prowess, hearing thus, the words of these damsels, thought within himself, thus :-- what shall I do under the above circumstances.
Ch 7 Verse 2 Verse
Sanskrit Text
हास्योऽहं मुनिवृन्देषु भविष्याम्यद्य सङ्गमात् । अहङ्कारादिदं प्राप्तं दुःखं नात्र विचारणा । मूलं धर्मविनाशस्य प्रथमं यदहङ्कृतिः ॥ २ ॥
English Translation
If I indulge now in sexual pleasures, I will be an object of laughter amongst the Munis. This present trouble has, no doubt, arisen from my Ahamkāra (egoism). This Ahamkāra is the first and foremost in ruining one’s Dharma.
Ch 7 Verse 3-5 Verse
Sanskrit Text
मूलं संसारवृक्षस्य यतः प्रोक्तो महात्मभिः । दृष्ट्वा मौनं समाधाय न स्थितोऽहं समागतम् ॥ ३ ॥ वाराङ्गनागणं जुष्टं तेनासं दुःखभाजनम् । उत्पादितास्तथा नार्यो मया धर्मव्ययेन वै ॥ ४ ॥ तास्तु मां बाधितुं वृत्ताः कामार्ताः प्रमदोत्तमाः । ऊर्णनाभिरिवाद्याहं जालेन स्वकृतेन वै ॥ ५ ॥
English Translation
The wise sages have declared this Ahamkāra as the root of this tree of world. I did not observe the vow of silence on seeing those damsels come here; I have held much conversations with them; therefore I have got into this troublesome anxiety and sorrow. I have created these damsels at the expense of my Dharma and Tapasyā. The beautiful and lovely damsels sent by Indra are now full of lust; and are bent on ruining my tapasayā. Now if through Ahamkāra I had not created the damsels, I would not have fallen into this difficulty. Now I am caught firmly in the meshes of my own creation like a spider; what am I to do next!
Ch 7 Verse 6-7 Verse
Sanskrit Text
बद्धोऽस्मि सुदृढेनात्र किं कर्तव्यमतः परम् । यदि चिन्तां समुत्सृज्य सन्त्यजाम्यबला इमाः ॥ ६ ॥ शप्त्वा भ्रष्टा व्रजिष्यन्ति सर्वा भग्नमनोरथाः । मुक्तोऽहं सञ्चरिष्यामि विजने परमं तपः ॥ ७ ॥
English Translation
If I abandon these damsels, thinking that there is no necessity to reconsider the matter, then these would be broken hearted; and they would go away cursing me? Yet I would be free from this present danger at least and then be able to practise excellent tapasyā in a lonely place. Therefore, now, I will get angry and tell these damsels go away from me.
Ch 7 Verse 8 Verse

English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- O King! The Muni Nārāyaṇa thought that he would become thus happy; but, at the next moment, he discussed in his mind thus :--
Ch 7 Verse 9 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विमर्शमकरोच्चित्ते सुखोत्पादनसाधने । द्वितीयोऽयं महाशत्रुः क्रोधः सन्तापकारकः ॥ ९ ॥
English Translation
The second great enemy is anger; it is greater than causing hurt to others; and it is is greater than lust aud avarice.
Ch 7 Verse 10 Verse
Sanskrit Text
कामादप्यधिको लोके लोभादपि च दारुणः । क्रोधाभिभूतः कुरुते हिंसां प्राणविघातिनीम् ॥ १० ॥
English Translation
Out of anger people commit murder; this murder is the source of hell and is giving pains to all.
Ch 7 Verse 11 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दुःखदां सर्वभूतानां नरकारामदीर्घिकाम् । यथाग्निर्घर्षणाज्जातः पादपं प्रदहेत्तथा ॥ ११ ॥
English Translation
As trees, rubbing against each other, generate fire; and are themselves burnt up in this fire, so fire arising from this body ultimately burns this body to death.
Ch 7 Verse 12 Verse
Sanskrit Text
देहोत्पन्नस्तथा क्रोधो देहं दहति दारुणः । व्यास उवाच । इति सञ्चिन्त्यमानं तं भ्रातरं दीनमानसम् ॥ १२ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- The younger brother Nara on seeing his elder brother anxious and low-spirited, spoke out what is right, as follows :--
Ch 7 Verse 13 Verse
Sanskrit Text
उवाच वचनं तथ्यं नरो धर्मसुतोऽनुजः । नर उवाच । नारायण महाभाग कोपं यच्छ महामते ॥ १३ ॥
English Translation
O Nārāyaṇa! You are very intelligent and very good; therefore reliquish this feeling of anger and betake to quietude and peace, and kill the dreadful anger.
Ch 7 Verse 14-15 Verse
Sanskrit Text
शान्तं भावं समाश्रित्य नाशयाहङ्कृतिं पराम् । पुराहङ्कारदोषेण तपो नष्टं किलावयोः ॥ १४ ॥ सङ्ग्रामश्चाभवत्ताभ्यां भावाभ्यामसुरेण ह । दिव्यवर्षसहस्रं तु प्रह्लादेन महाद्भुतम् ॥ १५ ॥
English Translation
Do you not remember that it is through this Ahamkāra and anger that our tapasyā was destroyed on a previous occasion; and we had to fight severely with Prahlāda, the Lord of the Asuras for one full divine thousand years.
Ch 7 Verse 16 Verse
Sanskrit Text
दुःखं बहुतरं प्राप्तं तत्रावाभ्यां सुरोत्तम । तस्मात्क्रोधं परित्यज्य शान्तो भव मुनीश्वर ॥ १६ ॥
English Translation
O Lord of the Devas! We were put to much difficulties then; therefore O Lord of the Munis! Get rid of this anger; be quiet! The sages declare the peace is the root cause and the only object of Tapasyā.
Ch 7 Verse 17 Verse
Sanskrit Text
(शान्तत्वं तपसो मूलं मुनिभिः परिकीर्तितं ।)॥ व्यास उवाच । इति तस्य वचः श्रुत्वा शान्तोऽभूद्धर्मनन्दनः । जनमेजय उवाच । संशयोऽयं मुनिश्रेष्ठ प्रह्लादेन महात्मना ॥ १७ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- On hearing these words of his younger brother Nara, the Dharma’s son Nārāyaṇa took to peace.
Ch 7 Verse 18 Verse
Sanskrit Text
विष्णुभक्तेन शान्तेन कथं युद्धं कृतं पुरा । कृतवन्तौ कथं युद्धं नरनारायणावृषी ॥ १८ ॥
English Translation
Janamejaya said :-- O Lord of the Munis! The high souled Prahlāda was a devotee of Viṣṇu and of a peace loving heart: how it was that, in the ancient days, the battle took place between him and these Ṛṣis; how could the Ṛṣis fight? There is this great doubt in my mind.
Ch 7 Verse 19-20 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तापसौ धर्मपुत्रौ द्वौ सुशान्तमानसावुभौ । समागमः कथं जातस्तयोर्दैत्यसुतस्य च ॥ १९ ॥ सङ्ग्रामस्तु कथं ताभ्यां कृतस्तेन महात्मना । प्रह्लादोऽप्यतिधर्मात्मा ज्ञानवान्विष्णुतत्परः ॥ २० ॥
English Translation
These two Dharma’s sons were ascetics and peace loving; how the fight could come to pass between these and the Daityāsuras? How did these two Ṛṣis fight with the high souled Prahlāda?
Ch 7 Verse 21-22 Verse
Sanskrit Text
नरनारायणौ तद्वत्तापसौ सत्त्वसंस्थितौ । तेन ताभ्यां समुद्भूतं वैरं यदि परस्परम् ॥ २१ ॥ तदा तपसि धर्मे च श्रम एव हि केवलम् । क्व जपः क्व तपश्चर्या पुरा सत्ययुगेऽपि च ॥ २२ ॥
English Translation
Prahlāda was very religious, full of knowledge and very much devoted to Viṣṇu. Nara Nārāyaṇa were Sattvik and ascetics; therefore if there had occurred enmity between those, it appears that the religion and asceticism, Tapasyā and Dharma were matters in name only; and the labour was spent in vain in the golden age even. What was the value of asceticism and meditation and muttering silently the mantras! No one can make out.
Ch 7 Verse 23 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तादृशैर्न जितं चित्तं क्रोधाहङ्कारसंवृतम् । न क्रोधो न च मात्सर्यमहङ्काराङ्कुरं विना ॥ २३ ॥
English Translation
Oh! Persons like them could not conquer their hearts full of anger and egoism! Anger and jealousy cannot spring unless there be at the bottom a feeling of egoism (Ahamkāra).
Ch 7 Verse 24 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अहङ्कारात्समुत्पन्नाः कामक्रोधादयः किल । वर्षकोटिसहस्रं तु तपः कृत्वातिदारुणम् ॥ २४ ॥
English Translation
All the passions, lust, greed, anger, etc., come out of Ahamkāra (egoism), there is no doubt of it; one hundred lakh years of severe asceticism are rendered quite useless by the cropping up afterwards of a bit of Ahamkāra.
Ch 7 Verse 25 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अहङ्काराङ्कुरे जाते व्यर्थं भवति सर्वथा । यथा सूर्योदये जाते तमोरूपं न तिष्ठति ॥ २५ ॥
English Translation
As darkness is dispelled entirely on sunrise, so no trace of religious merit can exist on the rising of a bit of Ahamkāra.
Ch 7 Verse 26 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अहङ्काराङ्कुरस्याग्रे तथा पुण्यं न तिष्ठति । प्रह्लादोऽपि महाभाग हरिणा समयुध्यत ॥ २६ ॥
English Translation
When Prahlāda could fight with Śrī Bhagavān Hari, then, Oh! all his merits in this world are rendered of no use whatsoever.
Ch 7 Verse 27 Verse
Sanskrit Text
तदा व्यर्थं कृतं सर्वं सुकृतं किल भूतले । नरनारायणौ शान्तौ विहाय परमं तपः ॥ २७ ॥
English Translation
Where is the religious merit and where is peace when the quiet souled persons Nara Nārāyaṇa, the two Ṛṣis began to fight, without paying any heed to their highest end, the Tapasyā?
Ch 7 Verse 28 Verse
Sanskrit Text
कृतवन्तौ यदा युद्धं क्व शमः सुकृतं पुनः । ईदृग्भ्यां सत्त्वयुक्ताभ्यामजेया यद्यहङ्कृतिः ॥ २८ ॥
English Translation
When Ahamkāra became invincible by the two Ṛṣis, then what can be expected from the weak trivial persons like us in the matter of subjugating this Ahamkāra?
Ch 7 Verse 29 Verse
Sanskrit Text
मादृशानाञ्ज का वार्ता मुनेऽहङ्कारसङ्क्षये । अहङ्कारपरित्यक्तो न कोऽप्यस्ति जगत्त्रये ॥ २९ ॥
English Translation
Who can be free from Ahamkāra in these three worlds, when the high souled persons like the above were not free from it? I am now quite confident that, in this Universe, no body was ever before free from Ahamkāra nor will there be any such in the distant future.
Ch 7 Verse 30 Verse
Sanskrit Text
न भूतो भविता नैव यस्त्यक्तस्तेन सर्वथा । मुच्यते लोहनिगडैर्बद्धः काष्ठमयैस्तथा ॥ ३० ॥
English Translation
One can be free if bound by an iron or a wooden chain; but when one is pierced by Ahamkāra, one can never become free from it.
Ch 7 Verse 31 Verse
Sanskrit Text
अहङ्कारनिबद्धस्तु न कदाचिद्विमुच्यते । अहङ्कारावृतं सर्वं जगत्स्थावरजङ्गमम् ॥ ३१ ॥
English Translation
This whole Universe, moving and unmoving, is rolling in this Samsāra (migration and transmigration) polluted by urine and faeces, being covered by Ahamkāra.
Ch 7 Verse 32 Verse
Sanskrit Text
भ्रमत्येव हि संसारे विष्ठामूत्रप्रदूषिते । ब्रह्मज्ञानं कुतस्तावत्संसारे मोहसंवृते ॥ ३२ ॥
English Translation
Where is, then the Brahmā Jñana? O Good One in vows! The Karma theory, according to the Mimāmsakas, seems reasonable and true.
Ch 7 Verse 33 Verse
Sanskrit Text
मतं मीमांसकानां वै सम्मतं भाति सुव्रत । महान्तोऽपि सदा युक्ताः कामक्रोधादिभिर्मुने ॥ ३३ ॥
English Translation
O Muni! What can you expect from the weak-minded persons like me in this Kali yuga, when the great persons are always overpowered with lust, anger, etc.
Ch 7 Verse 34-35 Verse
Sanskrit Text
मादृशानां कलावस्मिन्का कथा मुनिसत्तम । व्यास उवाच । कार्यं वै कारणाद्भिन्नं कथं भवति भारत ॥ ३४ ॥ कटकं कुण्डलञ्चैव सुवर्णसदृशं भवेत् । अहङ्कारोद्भवं सर्वं ब्रह्माण्डं सचराचरम् ॥ ३५ ॥
English Translation
Vyāsa said :-- O Descendant of Bharata! How can the effect be different from its cause? Gold and golden ear-rings though different in form owing to upādhis, are both similar to their original cause, the metal gold. Thread is the cause of cloth; therefore as cloth cannot be different from its thread, so this whole universe, moving and unmoving, is sprung from Ahamkāra; then how can it be free from Ahamkāra?
Ch 7 Verse 36 Verse
Sanskrit Text
पटस्तन्तुवशः प्रोक्तस्तद्वियुक्तः कथं भवेत् । मायागुणैस्त्रिभिः सर्वं रचितं स्थिरजङ्गमम् ॥ ३६ ॥
English Translation
All this, moving and unmoving, including a blade of grass, are fashioned out of the three qualities of Māyā; so if it be formed of those qualities, what repentance can come to those who are wise and know every phenomenon as unreal?
Ch 7 Verse 37 Verse
Sanskrit Text
सतृणस्तम्बपर्यन्तं का तत्र परिदेवना । ब्रह्मा विष्णुस्तथा रुद्रस्ते चाहङ्कारमोहिताः ॥ ३७ ॥
English Translation
O Best of kings! Brahmā, Viṣṇu or Maheśa, even these are all rolling in this vast ocean of Samsāra, being bewildered and fascinated by Ahamkāra.
Ch 7 Verse 38 Verse
Sanskrit Text
भ्रमन्त्यस्मिन्महागाधे संसारे नृपसत्तम । वसिष्ठनारदाद्याश्च मुनयो ज्ञानिनः परम् ॥ ३८ ॥
English Translation
The great sages like Vaśiṣṭha, Nārada and the other Munis are frequently taking their births in this Samsāra.
Ch 7 Verse 39 Verse

English Translation
In this Trilokī, there is not even one embodied soul, who is entirely free from this Māyā and has become quiet and immersed in the high bliss of the Supreme Self.
Ch 7 Verse 40 Verse
Sanskrit Text

English Translation
O Best of kings! Lust, anger, avarice, and fascination, all, arise from Ahamkāra. These do not leave any embodied person.
Ch 7 Verse 41-42 Verse

English Translation
Studying all the Vedas and Purāṇas, going to all the sacred places pilgrimages, making charities, thinking on Paramātman and worshipping the gods, doing all these, the people still get attached to sensual objects and act like a thief.
Ch 7 Verse 43 Verse
Sanskrit Text

English Translation
O Son of Kuru! In the three yugas, the Satya, Tretā, Dwāpara, Dharma had been pierced and wounded very much; what to say of Dharma in this Kali Yuga!
Ch 7 Verse 44 Verse

विद्धोऽत्रास्ति च धर्मोऽपि का कथाद्य कलौ पुनः । स्पर्धा सदैव सद्रोहा लोभामर्षौ च सर्वदा ॥ ४४ ॥
English Translation
You will find quarrels, avarice, anger raging always in this Kali yuga. Therefore there is no wonder that you will not find any one thinking and doing what is worth thinking, and doing what is not worth doing?
Ch 7 Verse 45 Verse
Sanskrit Text

English Translation
Free from envy, anger, and jealousy, such persons are rare now-a-days in this Kali yuga. Some peaceful persons exist here and there to keep up the ideal.
Ch 7 Verse 46 Verse

English Translation
The king said :-- O Muni! They are blessed and holy who are free from this fascination of Māyā, self controlled, who have conquered their passions, and who follow good conduct. They have risen above the Trilokī.
Ch 7 Verse 47 Verse

English Translation
O Best of Munis! My high minded father put a dead serpent round the neck of an ascetic without any fault; I am very sorry to think of his act.
Ch 7 Verse 48 Verse
Sanskrit Text

English Translation
Therefore, O Muni ! Kindly suggest any means by which I can now redress that act. O Bhagavan! I do not know what will be the result of this act, committed out of the bewildering of intellect.

न जाने बुद्धिसम्मोहात्किं वा कार्यं भविष्यति । मधु पश्यति मूढात्मा प्रपातं नैव पश्यति ॥ ४९ ॥
English Translation
Fools in search of honey see only honey before them but not the falls, whence they might tumble down and die. So the stupid men do disgraceful acts and do not get afraid of the tortures of hell.

Ch 7 Verse 51 Verse
Sanskrit Text

English Translation
How was it that Prahlāda went out of Pātāla (the nether regions) and went to the great holy place, the hermitage of Badarikaśrama in the Sārasvata country, the great place for pilgrimage.

O Muni! What was it that led the best of the Munis, the two ascetics to fight with Prahlāda?

enmity springs where there is wealth, wife, or land. The two Maharṣis were desireless, had nothing of these; how, then, without any cause, they fought such a battle!
